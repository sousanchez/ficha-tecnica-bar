## 2. Kitchen Basics — Key Concepts

- **Two essential tools:** an accurate digital scale and a digital probe thermometer. The book insists on weighing (not measuring by volume) for all precision work, especially emulsifiers/gelling agents (volume measurement can be off by up to 15%).
- **Sous vide, four simple steps:** (1) season/prep food, (2) seal in a bag, (3) cook in a temperature-controlled water bath, (4) finish (sear/glaze/etc.).
- **Pressure cooking:** standard working pressure used throughout the book is a **gauge pressure of 1 bar / 15 psi**; start timing once full pressure is reached; depressurize quickly by running tepid water over the lid rim for most vegetable/quick recipes.
- **Lightly cooked food:** recipes cooked below FDA/USDA pasteurization temperatures are flagged "lightly cooked." Serve immediately; never hold as leftovers (pathogens multiply quickly below 50 °C/120 °F). Not recommended for immunocompromised, elderly, or very young diners.
- **Refrigeration safety:** keep the fridge at or below 5 °C/40 °F. It's actually safer to cool covered hot food briefly at room temperature (≤4 hours) or in an ice bath than to overload the fridge with hot pans.
- **Sous vide bag air removal:** water-displacement method for zip-top bags is used throughout as the default (vacuum sealers reserved for long-cook items to prevent flotation/oxidation).

### 3.6 Fish — Salmon (core temperature)

- **Rare (preferred):** 45 °C/113 °F — ~25 min for 2.5 cm/1 in fillets in a 46 °C/115 °F bath
- **Medium:** 48 °C/118 °F
- **Firm:** 51–52 °C/124–126 °F
- Kitchen-sink method: set tap water 2–5 °C (5–9 °F) above target temp; a large pot of water holds temperature ~1 h; a cooler holds it up to 5 h.
- Toaster-oven method: calibrate oven, set 90–110 °C/200–225 °F; cook to 45 °C/113 °F core, 38–40 min.
- Crispy fish/chicken/pork skin: cook skin sous vide (fish 5 h; chicken 12 h; pork 18 h), then deep-fry at **185 °C/365 °F**, 1–3 min, or bake at 165 °C/330 °F, 20–45 min for chips.

### 3.7 Egg Coagulation (core science, Ch. 6)

- Egg **yolk** proteins begin coagulating above **62 °C/144 °F**, progressively firming to fully hard-cooked at about **80 °C/176 °F**.
- French Scrambled Eggs are cooked sous vide at **72 °C/162 °F** for ~35 min, then blended smooth and aerated in a whipping siphon.
- A yolk cooked at 72 °C/162 °F for a set time becomes fully spherical/firm — the basis of the book's "modernist deviled egg" (whole cooked yolk sauced with a puree of the white).

### 3.10 Thickener & Gelling-Agent Ratios (Ch. 5 "Sauces")

| Agent | Ratio (per 100 g liquid) | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Xanthan gum | 0.1–0.15 g | thin sauce |
| Xanthan gum | 0.2–0.3 g | thick, ketchup/dressing-like |
| Agar powder | 0.25 g | thin broth |
| Agar powder | 0.4–0.5 g | moderately thick sauce |
| Agar powder | 0.9–1.1 g | puree-thick gel |
| Wondra (dusted, whisked not blended) | 4–5 g | classic gravy thickening |
| Liquid soy lecithin | 1–2 g | stabilizes/emulsifies a jus or pan sauce |

Quick-set fluid gel method: chill the hot, hydrated agar gel in an ice-water bath while blending with an immersion blender until it sets below **30 °C/86 °F**, yielding a pourable fluid gel.

Troubleshooting table (condensed) — Common thickening/gelling problems:
- **Lumps ("fish eyes")** → agent dispersed unevenly; dust it evenly while blending, or pre-mix with sugar/oil.
- **No set despite swollen granules** → steam caused early clumping or agent hit hot liquid before full dispersion; keep powder away from steam until dispersed.
- **Granules won't swell/dissolve** → too little gelling agent/starch weighed; use an accurate scale.
- **Gummy Wondra sauce** → Wondra was blended (not whisked) or overworked; whisk gently, don't over-blend.
- Enzymes in fresh kiwi/pineapple/papaya block gelatin — use cooked, canned, or frozen fruit instead.
- Gelatin tolerates up to 36% alcohol but tastes best at ≤10%; agar is the alcohol-tolerant, heat-stable (up to 85 °C/185 °F) choice for cold or boozy applications.

### 3.11 Gelatin Bloom-Strength Reference (Ch. 23)

| Type | Bloom rating | g per sheet |
|---|---|---|
| Bronze | 125–155 | 3.3 |
| Silver | 160 | 2.5 |
| Gold | 190–220 | 2.0 |
| Knox powdered (US standard) | 225 | n/a |
| Platinum | 235–265 | 1.7 |

Conversion formula: **M_B = M_A × (Bloom_A ÷ Bloom_B)**. Example: 2.6 g Knox (225 Bloom) ≈ 2.6 × 225 ÷ 160 = 3.7 g silver gelatin. Substitution shortcut: 1.3 g bronze or 1.1 g silver gelatin per 100 g liquid replaces Knox powdered gelatin.
Vegetarian panna cotta substitute: 0.8 g agar + 0.65 g xanthan gum, or 0.65 g iota + 0.5 g kappa carrageenan (per the recipe's liquid base).

### 3.12 Sodium Citrate Emulsified-Cheese Ratio (Ch. 19)

- **Mac and Cheese sauce:** 11 g sodium citrate + 265 g water/milk (≈4% of liquid) melts 285 g grated cheddar into a smooth, non-breaking sauce.
- **Perfectly Melting Cheese Slice — scaling rule:** weigh the liquid + grated cheeses together and use **2.8% of that total weight** in sodium citrate. Example: 750 g of finished cheese slices → 21 g sodium citrate.

### 3.13 Emulsifier/Fat Ratios — Mayonnaise & Hollandaise

- **Modernist Mayonnaise:** 75 g pasteurized egg yolk (67 °C/153 °F, 35 min sous vide) : 45 g water : 25 g Dijon : 300 g neutral oil : 18 g lemon juice : 10 g white wine vinegar (yolk = 100% baseline, oil = 400%).
- **Sous Vide Hollandaise:** wine/shallot/vinegar reduction blended with 75 g egg yolk, cooked sous vide at **65 °C/149 °F for 30 min**, then emulsified with 225 g melted butter (300% of yolk weight). 63 °C/145 °F bath → lighter foam; 67 °C/153 °F → denser foam when siphon-aerated with N₂O.

### 3.14 Weight, Volume & Temperature Conversion Notes

- °C→°F: multiply by 1.8, add 32. °F→°C: subtract 32, multiply by 5/9 (0.56).
- Grams↔ounces: divide/multiply by 28.35. 1 lb = 453.59 g/16 oz; 1 kg = 2.2 lb.
- Book policy: **all recipes are developed and tested by weight**, not volume — volume figures given are secondary/approximate (rounding alone can shift 20 mL between 3½ tsp and 1½ Tbsp depending on the source weight).
- Pressure unit note: gauge pressure of 1 bar = 15 psi is the pressure *above* ambient atmospheric (1.013 bar/14.5 psi at sea level) — this is the pressure-cooker standard used in every recipe.

## 4. Recipe Sampler by Category (exact ingredients & procedures)

### 4.1 Basics — Sauces & Emulsions

**Onion Fluid Gel** (yield 500 g/2 cups)
- Sweet onion 635 g (127%) · shallots 155 g (31%) · neutral oil 100 g (20%) · whole milk 500 g (100%) · **agar powder 3.5 g (0.7%)** · salt to taste
- Sweat onions/shallots in oil; bag with milk, cook sous vide 88 °C/190 °F for 1 h; sieve to make "onion milk"; whisk cold agar into 500 g onion milk, boil 1–2 min to hydrate, chill to set, then puree until smooth and fluid. Agar gels tolerate reheating up to 85 °C/185 °F without breaking (unlike gelatin).
- **Variation — Egg-Yolk Fluid Gel:** vacuum-seal 150 g/10 yolks, sous vide 80 °C/176 °F for 35 min; blend with 150 g flavored cream/stock; season with lemon.

**Pressure-Cooked Drippings** (yield 200 g/1 cup)
- Chicken wings 450 g (100%) + ground chicken thighs 450 g (100%) + soy sauce 9 g + honey 9 g, packed into 3 canning jars, lids loosened ¼ turn, pressure-cooked 1 bar/15 psi for 1 h in 2.5 cm of water. Strain; thicken with xanthan gum as needed (or a few cubes of cold butter); 1–2 g liquid soy lecithin further stabilizes the emulsion.

**Modernist Mayonnaise** (yield 450 g) — see §3.13 for ratios. Variations: **Aioli** (replace 150 g oil with Garlic Confit oil + 50 g confit garlic), **Bacon Mayonnaise** (rendered bacon fat + 0.7 g xanthan gum + 65 g crumbled bacon), **Rouille** (+59 g pimentón dulce + 0.5 g cayenne), **Tartar Sauce** (+35 g minced gherkins + 15 g parsley).

**Sous Vide Hollandaise** (yield 265 g/5 cups foam) — see §3.13. Aerated via 1 L whipping siphon + 2 N₂O cartridges; can be held warm in the siphon in a 55 °C/131 °F bath up to 1.5 h. Variations: Crustacean Hollandaise (crustacean butter), Garlic Hollandaise (+40 g garlic), Spicy Hollandaise (sesame oil + Korean Wing Sauce).

**Redeye Gravy** (yield 200 g/1 cup): sweet onion 80 g caramelized in pork fat, deglazed with Brown Pork Stock 500 g + maple syrup 15 g, reduced by two-thirds (~10 min), infused off-heat 5 min with whole French-roast coffee beans 25 g, finished with butter 35 g whisked in off the boil.

**Red Wine Glaze** (yield 100 g/⅓ cup, for Braised Short Ribs): ground beef 1.3 kg browned; mirepoix (onion/carrot/leek 500 g each) sautéed 30–35 min; tomato paste 50 g; red wine 750 g/1 bottle reduced to syrup (10–12 min); beef knucklebones 1.3 kg + water 1 kg + herbs pressure-cooked 1 bar/15 psi for 2 h; strained and reduced 45–60 min to a syrupy glaze.

**Blue Cheese Sauce** (600 g): Wondra 12 g + sodium citrate 4 g (1%) whisked into cold whole milk 200 g, simmered, then 400 g crumbled blue cheese blended in gradually with an immersion blender — sodium citrate again prevents breaking.

**Korean Wing Sauce** (280 g/1 cup): gochujang 135 g (100%) : sugar 50 g (37%) : soy sauce 30 g (22%) : Shaoxing wine 27 g (20%) : toasted sesame oil 20 g (15%) : garlic 10 g : ginger 8 g — whisked together and warmed.

**Chinese Garlic Chili Condiment** (145 g): neutral oil 432 g (288% of garlic) fries garlic 150 g until golden (~6 min), then scallion whites 40 g (~4 min); combined with jalapeño 33 g and salt 8 g. Serve within 30 min for crisp texture.

### 4.2 Eggs

**French Scrambled Eggs** (380 g, 4–6 servings): whole eggs 200 g (100%) + egg yolks 60 g (30%) + melted butter 60 g (30%) + whole milk 60 g (30%) + salt 4 g (2%), blended smooth, bagged, cooked sous vide at **72 °C/162 °F for ~35 min**, then re-blended and optionally aerated in a whipping siphon.

### 4.3 Beef & Steak

**Sous Vide Steak** (4 servings, 1 kg/2 strip steaks): preheat bath **55 °C/131 °F**; bag steaks with a little oil; cook to core temp **54 °C/129 °F, ~50 min**; deep-fry in 225 °C/437 °F oil ~30 sec to crisp crust, or blowtorch-sear; finish with melted butter and Maldon salt.

**Modernist Hamburger Patty** (4 patties, 200 g each): rib eye 360 g + short-rib meat 360 g + hanger steak 80 g, ground separately through a 4.5 mm plate; **salt exactly 0.8% of meat weight (6.4 g here), folded in exactly 1 hour before cooking**; formed into 2.5 cm-thick square patties; sous vide **55 °C/131 °F to core 54 °C/129 °F, ~45 min**; deep-fried in 220 °C/428 °F oil ~30 sec/side for crust. Firm variation adds 15 g egg yolk per 400 g meat.

**Braised Short Ribs** (4 servings, 600 g): bone-in/boneless short ribs 1.2 kg, vacuum-sealed, cooked sous vide **62 °C/144 °F for 72 h** (or 58 °C/136 °F/72 h for a steak-like medium-rare texture); sliced from bone, basted 1 min in simmering Red Wine Glaze (200 g, 17% of meat weight).

**Potato Puree** (4–6 servings, 630 g): Yukon Gold potatoes 500 g cut 2.5 cm, sous vide **70 °C/158 °F for 35 min** (gelatinizes/stabilizes starch without fully cooking), then simmered in fresh water 25 min, riced, mixed with **butter 200 g (40%) and cream/milk 125 g (25%)** — roughly a 2:5 potato-to-fat+liquid ratio (classic French recipes go up to 1:2). Never use a food processor (ruptures starch granules → gluey texture).

### 4.4 Pork

**Pressure-Cooked Carnitas** (6–8 servings, 1 kg): pork shoulder cubes 1.3 kg + Brown Pork Stock 460 g, pressure-cooked **1 bar/15 psi for 30 min**; liquid reduced to syrup (~25 min) with achiote paste 15 g + chipotle powder; pork returned and glazed 6 min. **Achiote Paste:** annatto seeds 78 g, Pressure-Cooked Garlic Confit 40 g, habanero 25 g, black pepper 7 g, coriander/cumin seeds, orange juice 124 g, white wine vinegar 119 g, lemon juice 50 g, tequila 4 g. Sous vide variation: 65 °C/149 °F for 36 h.

### 4.5 Poultry

**Turkey Confit** (4 servings, 450 g): turkey legs 900 g cured with salt 40 g (4.4%) + sugar 4 g (0.4%), then sous vide at **60 °C/140 °F** sealed with 150 g (17%) cold duck fat and rosemary. Duck fat may be substituted with butter or neutral oil.

### 4.6 Fish & Shellfish

**Fragrant Sous Vide Salmon** (4 servings, 600 g): brine (1 kg water : 50 g salt 5% : 40 g sugar 4%) for 3–5 h (or halve salt/sugar and brine 24 h for a gentler cure); sous vide bath **46 °C/115 °F**, cook to core **45 °C/113 °F, ~25 min** for 2.5 cm fillets; basted in butter (80 g) with Fish Spice Mix for 30 sec/side. Firmer variant: cook to 51 °C/124 °F.

**Crispy Fish Skin**: scaled and cleaned skin vacuum-sealed, sous vide 5 h, iced, cut into 5×7.5 cm pieces, deep-fried at **185 °C/365 °F for 1–3 min**. Chicken skin: sous vide 12 h; pork skin: 18 h, before frying.

**Lobster prep ("How To Kill and Shell a Lobster"):** cut through the head cross behind the eyes; blanch tails 90 sec, claws 4 min, then ice bath; freeze-first alternative puts live lobster to sleep 30 min before a 2-minute kill-blanch.

**Shellfish sous vide bath preheat:** set 1 °C/2 °F above the highest target temperature in the batch, then cook to individual core temps per §3.5 table.

### 4.7 Pasta, Grains & Pizza

**Fresh Egg Noodles** (4 servings, 640 g): all-purpose flour 420 g (100%) : water 125 g (30%) : egg 50 g (12%) : olive oil 25 g (6%) : vital wheat gluten 16 g (4%, Bob's Red Mill) : salt 4 g (1%). Mixed 8 min in a stand mixer, rested 1 h+ vacuum-sealed, rolled to 2 mm sheets, cut, boiled in water salted at **0.5–0.75 g salt per 100 g water** for 30 sec (firm) or 60 sec (tender).

**Pressure-Cooked Carrots and Leeks**: 2.5 cm carrots/leeks on a rack above 1 cm water, pressure-cooked **1 bar/15 psi for 5 min**.

**Neapolitan Pizza Dough** (800 g, four 30–35 cm pizzas): Antico Caputo 00 flour 500 g (100%) : water 310 g (**62% hydration**) : honey 10 g (2%) : salt 10 g (2%) : vital wheat gluten 2.5 g (0.5%) : active dry yeast 2.5 g (0.5%). Mixed 5 min, rested 10 min, mixed 5 min more, divided into 200 g balls, proofed 1 h at room temp (or overnight refrigerated). Variations: Quinoa dough (250 g AP + 250 g quinoa flour, gluten increased to 5 g); Buckwheat dough (substitute 150 g buckwheat flour, gluten to 5 g); Breadsticks (55 g dough pieces, baked 220 °C/430 °F, 12–15 min).

**Risotto/Paella method** (see §3.9 table): sauté aromatics, toast rice 1–2 min until translucent, add stock/wine, pressure-cook 1 bar/15 psi to al dente, quick-depressurize. Parcook-and-freeze technique allows finishing in 2–5 min at service time.

### 4.8 Cheese

**Mac and Cheese** (5 servings, 800 g): water or milk 265 g (93%) + sodium citrate 11 g (4%) simmered, then white cheddar 285 g (100%) blended in gradually; tossed with 240 g (84%) boiled elbow macaroni.

**Perfectly Melting Cheese Slice** (500 g/12–14 slices): cold wheat beer or water 115 g (57.5%) + sodium citrate 14 g (7%) simmered; Gruyère 200 g (100%) + sharp cheddar 180 g (90%) melted in gradually; poured onto a warm sheet, chilled 2 h+, cut into 7.5 cm rounds. Scaling rule in §3.12.

### 4.9 Vegetables & Microwave

**Microwaved Black Cod with Scallions and Ginger** (2 servings, 450 g): black cod fillets 450 g topped with scallion/ginger 25 g+10 g and Shaoxing wine 5 g, microwaved at 800 W until opaque and flaking, **3¾–5 min**; finished with soy-sesame mixture and 190 °C/374 °F hot peanut oil drizzled over just before serving. Substitutable with halibut, snapper, rockfish, trout, black sea bass, pomfret, or sole.

**Microwaved Potato Salad**: fingerling potatoes 400 g + water 15 g microwaved at 1,100 W until tender, ~4 min; sliced, dressed with red onion, Bacon Mayonnaise, Dijon mustard.

### 4.10 Custards, Creams & Desserts

**Coffee Crème Brûlée** (4 servings, 330 g): heavy cream 150 g + whole milk 150 g cold-infused overnight with whole dark-roast coffee beans 80 g (retains volatile aromas lost by hot-infusing); strained 200 g blended with egg yolks 80 g (53%), sugar 50 g (33%), salt 0.3 g; sous vide bath **82 °C/180 °F**, custard cooked to core **80 °C/176 °F, ~1 h**; chilled 2 h; torched sugar crust. Variations: Pot de Crème (skip torching), Bain-Marie version (162 °C/325 °F oven, 25–30 min), Flan (substitute whole milk for cream + whole eggs for yolks).

**Sous Vide Lemon Curd** (6–8 servings, 850 g): egg yolks 120 g (30%) cooked sous vide **65 °C/149 °F for 35 min**, iced; blended with a sugar 300 g (75%) + water 40 g + citric acid 8 g (2%) syrup, then emulsified with butter 400 g (100%) cubed in gradually. Substitutes: grapefruit/mandarin/orange/lime juice for fruit-curd variants.

**Raspberry Panna Cotta**: milk bloomed with **Knox powdered gelatin (225 Bloom)** per the ratio in §3.11, blended with cream/raspberry purée/sugar/salt, strained, set 5–8 h refrigerated (~60 g per serving, 6 servings).

## 5. Glossary — Representative Sample (full glossary spans pages IV–XI)

- **Amylose/amylopectin** — the two glucose polymers in starch; amylose gels better, amylopectin thickens/binds water better (basis of why short-grain rice works for risotto but long-grain doesn't).
- **Bloom** — gelatin gel-strength rating (see §3.11).
- **Calcium chloride / calcium lactate** — mineral salts used to firm bean/pickle skins and in Modernist spherification gels.
- **Carrageenan** — seaweed-derived gelling alternative to gelatin: iota = soft/elastic gel, kappa = firm/brittle gel, lambda = thickener only (no gel).
- **Combi oven** — oven combining convection (fan-driven hot air) with injected steam.
- **Core temperature** — the temperature at the thickest part's center, monitored by probe thermometer; central concept of the whole book.
- **Gauge pressure** — pressure above ambient atmosphere (1 bar/15 psi standard used in every pressure-cooker recipe here).
- **Sodium citrate** — citric-acid salt; emulsifying salt central to the book's melting-cheese technique (mac & cheese, cheese slices).
- **Wondra** — instant-dissolving flour used as a modern substitute for classical roux thickening (4–5 g per 100 g liquid).
- **Xanthan gum** — bacterial-fermentation thickener used in tiny doses (0.1–0.3 g/100 g) with no perceptible flavor impact.

## 6. Food Safety Notes (About the Recipes, p. xxiv–xxv)

- USDA/FDA guidelines are temperature-only; true pathogen kill requires **time-and-temperature combinations** — the book calculates both.
- Example correction: USDA's recommended pork cooking temp dropped from 71 °C/160 °F to **63 °C/145 °F** after review.
- "Lightly cooked" recipes (fish, some eggs) fall below pasteurization guidelines by design, for texture — eat immediately, discard leftovers, avoid serving to immunocompromised/elderly/very young.
- Refrigerator target: below **5 °C/40 °F**.

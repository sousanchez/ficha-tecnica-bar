# Spritz: Italy's Most Iconic Aperitivo Cocktail, with Recipes

Talia Baiocchi (founder/editor of *PUNCH*) and Leslie Pariseau co-wrote this cultural and recipe history of the spritz, built around a reporting trip across northern Italy (Venice, Milan, Turin) interviewing bitters producers, prosecco makers, bar owners, and drinks historians. The book blends deep-dive cultural history (Greek/Roman wine-dilution rituals, Habsburg-era Austrian soldiers, the rise of Aperol and Select, the Futurist cocktail movement, the 1990s prosecco-and-ice revolution) with a curated recipe collection split into Classic, Modern, and Cousin categories, plus an aperitivo food chapter.

  - Sidebar: "The Cocktails of the Italian Futurists" (1909–1944 Futurist mixology/*polibibita*, categorizing drinks as "war in bed" vs. "peace in bed")
  - "Bitter Into Wine": Aperol (1919) and Select (1920) enter the market; regional bitter loyalties (Select in Venice, Aperol in Padua, Cappelletti in Brescia)
  - The 1990s shift to prosecco-and-ice on the beaches around Venice (Bar Capannina, Lido di Jesolo) and Aperol's 1990s–2007 marketing campaign that turned a regional ritual into "Aperol Spritz," a global phenomenon

  - **Modern** recipes (bartender-created riffs from craft cocktail bars worldwide)
  - **Cousins** (spritz-adjacent low-alcohol, bubbly, bitter drinks that diverge from the core formula — Americano-family drinks, fizzes, coolers)

## Philosophy and Technique Synthesis

**The three rules of "How to Spritz."** The authors distill the entire category to three non-negotiables:
1. **Always effervescent** — via soda water, prosecco, another sparkling wine, or a flavoured soda; a spritz without bubble isn't a spritz.
2. **Low in alcohol** — no more than about 1 oz of strong spirits (preferably less); it's a drink for the liminal hour between work and play, not for getting drunk.
3. **A pre-dinner drink** — meant to be bitter, opening the stomach for a meal, consumed in the specific window between work and play (the *aperitivo* hour).

**The archetypal ratio.** The "spritz archetype" that emerged by the late 20th century is roughly **3 parts prosecco : 2 parts bitter liqueur : 1 part soda**, built over ice, always assembled in the same order in Venice — ice first, then prosecco, then bitters, then soda — a sequence the authors describe as "not a joking matter" regionally.

**Regional identity is embedded in the bitter.** Which bitter liqueur goes into the spritz is a matter of local allegiance almost like soccer-club loyalty: Select in Venice, Aperol in Padua, Cappelletti Aperitivo (aka Specialino) in Brescia, and so on. The book treats this variability as a feature, not a bug — a way of reading a region's identity through its glass.

**Category logic (Classic / Modern / Cousins).**
- *Classic* = the traditional canon along the actual "spritz trail" (Trieste to Turin): wine or prosecco + soda + citrus + a bitter (Aperol, Campari, or sometimes an amaro like Cynar), always finished with a signature regional garnish.
- *Modern* = bartender-created riffs (from bars like Attaboy, Death & Co, Nitecap, The NoMad, Maison Premiere) that keep the classic template's proportions and spirit but substitute in craft ingredients — fresh juices, housemade syrups/shrubs, infused vermouths, unconventional bitters.
- *Cousins* = drinks that share the spritz's bitter/bubbly/low-alcohol DNA but diverge structurally — using egg whites, beer, muddled fruit, or a Collins/sour build instead of the straight three-ingredient assembly (the Americano and its riffs, Sgroppino, various fizzes).

**Substitution philosophy.** The authors explicitly encourage flexibility: swap Cocchi Americano for bianco vermouth, lime juice for lemon juice, tonic water for quinquina wine, etc. — "the true spirit of the spritz requires a bit of homegrown curiosity."

**Prosecco quality matters.** Since prosecco typically makes up the bulk of a spritz's volume, the book insists that "the better the prosecco, the better the drink," recommending Charmat-method producers (Nino Franco's Rustico, Sorelle Bronca's Extra Dry/Brut, Adami's Garbèl and Bosco di Gica) and flagging the more traditional, lees-retaining *col fondo* style (Casa Coste Piane, Ca dei Zago, Bele Casel, Zanotto) as a more serious, avant-garde expression of the region.

## Component Library (Syrups & Shrubs, referenced across the recipe chapter)

- **Simple Syrup** — 1 cup sugar : 1 cup water, heated on very low heat until dissolved (never boiled), rested 30 minutes
- **Honey Syrup** — 1 cup honey : ¼ cup water, warmed until dissolved
- **Ginger Syrup** — ½ cup fresh ginger juice : ½ cup sugar, warmed until dissolved
- **Lemon Syrup** — 1 cup fresh lemon juice : 1 cup sugar
- **Raspberry Syrup** — 1 cup raspberries, 2 cups sugar, 1 cup water, crushed and heated gently (not boiled)
- **Grape Syrup** — 2 cups purple slipskin grapes, 1 cup sugar, 1 cup water, crushed and simmered
- **Vanilla Syrup** — 1 cup sugar, 1 cup water, 1 split vanilla pod, simmered together
- **Cranberry Shrub** — 1 cup apple cider vinegar, 1 cup water, 1 cup sugar, ¼ cup frozen cranberries, simmered 10 minutes, crushing the berries

## Recipe Sample — Organized by Chapter

### Classic

**Venetian Spritz** (rocks or wine glass; garnish: olive + orange half-wheel) — 2 oz bitter liqueur (Aperol or, more traditionally, Select), 3–4 oz prosecco, 2 oz soda water, built over ice. *Note: for a more bracingly bitter version, split the bitter liqueur 1:1 Aperol/Campari.*

**Negroni Sbagliato** (rocks glass; garnish: orange half-wheel) — 1 oz Campari, 1 oz sweet vermouth, 3 oz prosecco, built over ice. (Claimed birthplace: Bar Basso, Milan, invented "by accident" in the early 1970s by Mirko Stocchetto, substituting prosecco for gin.)

**White Spritz / Spritz Liscio** (wine glass; garnish: seasonal citrus, herbs, or fruit) — 4 oz dry white wine, 2 oz soda water, an optional splash (~¼–½ oz) of homemade Lemon Syrup or fruit liqueur, built over ice.

**Bicicletta** (wine glass; garnish: lemon half-wheel) — 1–2 oz Campari, 3 oz white wine, soda water to top, built over ice. (Lombardia, 1930s; named for the bicycle its drinkers wobble home on.)

### Modern

**Rosé All Day** (Gabriel Orta & Elad Zvi, Broken Shaker, Miami) — 2 oz rosé, 1 oz Cocchi Americano, 1 oz Papaya Shrub, ½ oz fresh lemon juice, shaken and strained into a wine glass over ice, topped with 1 oz prosecco. *Papaya Shrub: 5–8 chunks papaya, 1 cup rice wine vinegar, ¼ cup sugar, simmered 20 minutes.*

**Tarocco Spritz** (rocks glass; garnish: blood orange wheel) — 1 oz gin, ½ oz Cappelletti, ½ oz fresh blood orange juice, ½ oz fresh lemon juice, ½ oz Vanilla Syrup, shaken with ice, strained over fresh ice, topped with 2 oz prosecco.

**The Rib Tickler** (Alex Day, Nitecap, NYC) — 2 oz Dolin dry vermouth, ½ oz Suze, ½ oz St-Germain, ½ oz fresh lemon juice, ½ oz Simple Syrup, built in a wine glass over ice, topped with soda water. (A bubbly riff on the White Negroni.)

**Viking Culture** (Natasha David, created for Sunshine Co., Brooklyn) — 2 oz Caraway-Infused Cocchi Americano, ½ oz fresh lemon juice, ½ oz Simple Syrup, 1 tablespoon peach conserve (preferably Bonne Maman), shaken with ice, double-strained into a coupe, topped with sparkling wine. *Caraway-Infused Cocchi Americano: 2 tablespoons caraway seeds infused in a 750 ml bottle of Cocchi Americano for 2 hours, strained.*

**Alpine Spritz** (double rocks or wine glass; garnish: rosemary sprig) — ¾ oz Braulio, 1 oz Riesling, ½ oz Grape Syrup, 3 oz Crémant d'Alsace, built over ice.

**Nero Chinato Spritz** (Daniel Zacharczuk, The Varnish, LA) — 4 blackberries muddled in a Collins glass; add ice, ¾ oz fresh lemon juice, 2½ oz Cocchi Americano; top with equal parts Champagne/sparkling wine and soda water; garnish with 2–3 skewered blackberries.

**Grab Orange** (Matthew Biancaniello, LA) — 3 oz sparkling dry rosé, ½ oz Cappelletti, 1 oz Reduced Blood Orange Juice, built over ice in a flute or wine glass. *Reduced Blood Orange Juice: 1.1 litres blood orange juice simmered down to 1 cup.*

**Punch House Spritz** — 2 oz Cocchi Americano, 4 oz Lini Lambrusco Rosato, 1 oz fresh grapefruit juice, 1 oz soda water, built over ice. *Large-batch: 3 cups lambrusco rosé, 1½ cups Cocchi Americano, ⅔ cup grapefruit juice in a pitcher, divided among 4–6 glasses, topped with 1–2 oz soda water each.*

**Appennine Spritz** (Adam Bernbach, 2 Birds 1 Stone, Washington DC) — 1 oz Cocchi Barolo Chinato, 1 oz Cappelletti, 2 oz cava (if substituting prosecco, add ½ oz lemon juice), built over ice.

**Hugo Spritz** — ½ oz St-Germain and a mint sprig muddled together in a wine glass, rested 3 minutes; add ice, 4 oz prosecco, 1 oz soda water; stir gently. Garnish with mint sprig and lemon wheel. (South Tyrol; the traditional local base is *sciroppo di sambuco*/elderflower syrup, called *acqua santa*.)

**Amarena Spritz** — 2–3 brandied Amarena cherries (or 3 fresh stoned cherries) muddled with 1 teaspoon high-quality balsamic vinegar in a Collins or rocks glass; add 1 oz Punt e Mes, 1 oz Carpano Bianco, ice; top with 2 oz prosecco and 2 oz soda water.

**Byrrh It's Cold Outside** (Natasha David, Nitecap) — ¾ oz Byrrh Grand Quinquina, ¾ oz Lustau Amontillado sherry, ½ oz Ancho-Infused Moscatel sherry, ½ oz Cranberry Shrub, ½ oz fresh lemon juice, shaken briefly with ice, strained over crushed ice into a Collins glass, topped with 1½ oz sparkling wine.

**Mai Tai Spritz** (Martin Cate, Smuggler's Cove, San Francisco) — ½ oz fresh lime juice, ½ oz orgeat, ½ oz Pierre Ferrand Dry Curaçao, 1 oz Denizen Merchant's Reserve rum (or Appleton Estate Reserve) combined and chilled 1–2 hours; poured into a Collins glass filled with ice and topped with 4 oz chilled Brut Champagne.

**Spritzz** (Jim Meehan, PDT, NYC — created for Caffè della Posta, Bolgheri, Italy, 2011) — 2 oz Pellegrino Aranciata, ½ oz Zwack amaro, 2 oz prosecco, built over ice in a rocks glass.

**Viva Alberti** (Will Elliott, Maison Premiere, Brooklyn) — 1 oz Strega, ½ oz Cognac, 1 teaspoon Cappelletti, ½ oz fresh lemon juice, 3 dashes Angostura orange bitters, stirred with cracked ice in a wine glass, topped with high-quality sparkling wine.

**Wildair Spritz** (Jorge Riera, Wildair, NYC) — 2 oz Mauro Vergano Americano over 3 ice cubes in a wine glass, topped with Les Capriades Pet'Sec (or a col-fondo-style prosecco as substitute).

**Everything's Coming Up Rose** (Natasha David, Nitecap) — 2 oz dry rosé, 1 oz Lillet Rosé, 1 oz hibiscus tea, ½ oz fresh lemon juice, ½ oz Simple Syrup, ¼ oz Aperol, poured over ice in a wine glass, topped with more ice and 1 oz sparkling wine. Garnish with lemon wheel, grapefruit slice, and edible flowers.

**Safe Passage** (Keneniah Bystrom, Essex, Seattle) — 1 oz Amaro Nardini, ½ oz Aperol, ½ oz fresh lemon juice, ⅛ oz Castelvetrano olive brine, shaken with ice, strained into a chilled coupe, topped with 2½ oz prosecco, garnished with 2 Castelvetrano olives.

**Aperol Betty** (Terroni, Los Angeles) — 2 oz Aperol, 1 oz fresh orange juice, ½ oz fresh grapefruit juice, shaken with ice, strained over ice into a rocks or Collins glass, topped with 3 oz prosecco.

### Cousins

**Americano** (Collins glass; garnish: orange half-wheel) — 1½ oz Campari, 1½ oz sweet vermouth, built over ice, topped with soda water. (First documented in Ferruccio Mazzon's *Guida al Barman*, 1920 — the precursor to the Negroni.)

**Americano Perfecto** (Damon Boelte, Grand Army Bar, Brooklyn) — 1½ oz Campari, ¾ oz Dolin Rouge, ¾ oz Carpano Antica, built over ice in a Collins glass, slowly topped with 4 oz pilsner.

**Coffee Spritz** — 2 oz soda water poured into a Collins glass; separately combine ¾ oz Averna, ½ oz aged rum (preferably Flor de Caña 12), ½ oz Vanilla Syrup, 1¼ oz cold-brewed coffee, and 1 egg white, dry-shake, then shake with ice; strain into the glass, let sit 10 seconds, top with prosecco until foam rises above the rim.

**Punch Romaine** (adapted from the *Stork Club Bar Book*; snifter or rocks glass; garnish: orange peel) — 1 egg white, 1 oz white rum, ½ oz Simple Syrup, ½ oz fresh lemon juice, 1 oz fresh orange juice, dry-shaken then shaken with ice, strained over crushed ice, topped with Champagne or sparkling wine. (Served as the sixth course of the *Titanic*'s final first-class dinner.)

**Sensa Spritz** (Collins glass; garnish: cucumber ribbon or spear) — a pinch of sea salt and ½ oz Simple Syrup muddled with 2 cucumber slices in a shaker; add ¾ oz Salers, ¾ oz Dolin dry vermouth, ½ oz fresh lime juice, shake with ice, strain into a Collins glass over ice, top with soda water.

**Shore Leave Spritz** (Matthew Belanger, Donna, Brooklyn) — 1½ oz Cocchi Americano, ½ oz Perry's Tot navy-strength gin, ¾ oz fresh lime juice, ¾ oz fresh grapefruit juice, ½ oz Honey Syrup, ½ teaspoon Vanilla Syrup, ½ teaspoon allspice dram, shaken briefly with ice, strained over crushed ice into a hurricane glass, topped with 1 oz soda water.

**Rome with a View** (Michael McIlroy, Attaboy, NYC) — 1 oz Campari, 1 oz dry vermouth, 1 oz fresh lime juice, ¾ oz Simple Syrup, shaken with ice, strained over ice into a Collins glass, topped with soda water.

**Sippy Cup** (Leo Robitschek, The NoMad, NYC) — ¾ oz Ginger Syrup, 1 oz fresh lime juice, 1 oz Cocchi Vermouth di Torino, 1½ oz Averna, shaken with ice, strained into a highball over ice; add 2 dashes Angostura bitters and top with 1 oz soda water.

**White Port & Tonic** (highball glass; garnish: lemon half-wheel and mint sprig) — 2 oz white port, 4 oz tonic water (preferably Fever Tree), ½ oz fresh lemon juice, built over ice. *Variation: muddle mint leaves with orange bitters at the bottom of the glass before topping with port and tonic.*

**Tinto de Verano** (snifter glass) — 3 oz full-bodied, dry red wine and 1 oz Lemon Syrup shaken gently with ice, poured over ice into a tumbler or wine glass, topped with soda water.

**Sgroppino** (small rocks glass; garnish: mint sprig) — 1½ oz citrus vodka (preferably St. George), ½ cup lemon sorbet, 1 oz prosecco, whipped together with a whisk in a shaker until smooth and uniform, poured into a small rocks glass.

**Mont Blanc Fizz** (Dan Sabo, Ace Hotel, LA) — 1 egg white, 1¼ oz Amaro Meletti, ½ oz Grand Marnier, ¾ oz fresh lemon juice, ¼ oz orgeat, 2 dashes Angostura bitters, dry-shaken then shaken with crushed and cubed ice until very cold; strained into a highball glass, rested 10 seconds, topped with soda water.

**Cold in the Shadows** (Pamela Wiznitzer, Seamstress, NYC) — 1 oz Campari, ½ oz St. George raspberry liqueur, ½ oz Honey Syrup, 1 oz fresh lime juice, shaken quickly with ice; add 1½ oz Anderson Valley IPA; strain over crushed ice into a highball glass.

**Diamond Spritz Fizz** (Isaac Shumway, Tosca, San Francisco) — 1½ oz Aperol, ½ oz fresh lemon juice, ½ oz Dolin dry vermouth, ½ oz Honey Syrup, ¼ oz Gran Classico bitter amaro, 1 teaspoon fresh orange juice, 1 egg white, dry-shaken then shaken very hard (20 seconds) with ice; strained over a small amount of ice into a Collins glass, slowly topped with 2½ oz sparkling wine.

**Sangrita** (adapted from the *Stork Club Bar Book*; highball glass) — 2 oz red wine, 1 oz fresh pineapple juice, ¾ oz Simple Syrup, ¾ oz fresh lime juice, built over ice, topped with soda water.

**Hungry Hungry Hipster** (Toby Maloney, The Violet Hour, Chicago) — 2 oz Zwack amaro, 1 oz fresh orange juice, ¾ oz fresh lemon juice, shaken with ice, strained over ice into a Collins glass, topped with Miller High Life.

**Pink Lemonade à la Playboy** — 2 oz rosé, 1 oz fresh lemon juice, 1 oz fresh orange juice, ½ oz rosé/pink grapefruit liqueur, built over ice in a Collins glass or large tumbler, topped with soda water.

**Tunnel Vision** (Alex Day, Death & Co, NYC) — 2 oz Basil-Infused Dolin Blanc Vermouth, ½ oz pear eau de vie, ½ oz fresh lime juice, ½ oz Simple Syrup, 2 dashes absinthe, shaken with ice, strained into a Collins glass over ice, topped with soda water. *Basil-Infused Dolin Blanc: 8–10 basil leaves infused in a 750 ml bottle for 2 hours, strained.*

**Second Serve** (Dan Greenbaum, Attaboy, NYC) — 1 oz Amaro Montenegro, 1 oz fino sherry, 1 oz fresh lime juice, ¾ oz Simple Syrup, shaken and strained into a Collins glass over ice, topped with soda water.

**Bitter Intentions** (Bobby Heugel, Anvil / The Pastry War, Houston) — 1½ oz soda water poured into a Collins glass without ice; separately shake 2 oz Campari, ¾ oz fresh lemon juice, ¾ oz Simple Syrup with ice and strain into the glass over ice; add cracked ice and top with 1 oz vermouth (preferably Carpano Antica).

## Food Sample (The Aperitivo Table)

**Sarde in Saor** (adapted from Polpo, London; serves 6–8) — floured, pan-fried small sardines layered in a container with sweated onions marinated in white wine vinegar, toasted pine nuts, and raisins; refrigerated 2 days (keeps up to 5) and served at room temperature with toasted bread.

**Caffè Florian's Tramezzini** (makes 8) — crustless white sandwich bread spread with mayonnaise, layered with sliced hard-boiled egg and thin-sliced *parma cotto* (cooked ham), cut into triangles and skewered in pairs.

**Aperitivo Essenziale** — a house snack spread of Oven-Roasted Olives (mixed olives roasted at 240°C/465°F with olive oil, finished with orange zest), Saffron Almonds (Marcona almonds toasted and tossed in saffron-infused oil), and Hand-Cut Potato Chips (mandolined russet potatoes fried at 180°C/350°F, salted).

**Grissini with Prosciutto and Pickled Radicchio** (adapted from Polpo, London; makes 20) — radicchio leaves quick-pickled in boiled white wine/vinegar/juniper berry brine, wrapped with prosciutto around Italian breadsticks.

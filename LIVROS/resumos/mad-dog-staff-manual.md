# The Mad Dog — Staff Manual

**Source file:** `Copyofmaddogstaffmanual.pdf` (14 pages)
**Title page:** "The Mad Dog — what we do to make our guests happy, OR the staff manual" — September 2020, The Mad Dog Social Club

## Structural overview

A short, plainspoken staff manual (not a recipe book) for a single cocktail bar called The Mad Dog. It has no chapters/numbering; it reads as a continuous set of policy sections followed by an appendix. Rough structure:

1. Welcome / mission statement and core philosophy
2. List of specific staff responsibilities
3. Uniform and cleanliness
4. Punctuality
5. Dropbox (internal file sharing) and Rota (shift schedule)
6. Breaks/pauses
7. Complaints handling
8. Staff visiting as guests / serving friends & regulars
9. Music
10. Age and guest behavior (no minors, overservice)
11. "Relations with guests" — floor etiquette
12. "Service routine" — the numbered 7-step guest journey
13. "Features that make the difference between a poor job and a good job"
14. Opening/closing/cleaning procedures pointer (to a separate "Bar Book")
15. General notes of service and drink preparation (pour sizes, build order)
16. "Always simple | Never basic" philosophy statement
17. Reservations and reception management
18. Mad Dog Policy block: staff drinks, cigarettes, keep busy, phones, take-away, payment methods, outside food
19. Opening hours, shift end routine, dogs policy, behavior outside the venue
20. Useful information (emergency password, phone numbers, nearby transport, recommended bars/restaurants)
21. Conclusions
22. Appendix: "Supplements for training" — deeper reservation management rules and a "Floor: tips & tricks" section on active vs. reactive work, "pockets of time," priority levels, "never empty-handed," and pacing

## Core philosophy

- **Mission statement (bolded as the fundamental task):** "Provide professional, informed, courteous service to every guest at any time."
- Deliberately uses "guest" instead of "customer" — guests are to be welcomed as into one's own home.
- No hard distinction between "bartender" and "waiter" — both floor and bar roles are expected to reach a shared skill level; the manual uses "bartender" for both for simplicity.
- The manual teaches "our way," not generic bartending — it explicitly is not trying to teach the job from scratch, and invites feedback at monthly staff meetings.
- Explicitly separates *technique* (teachable) from *personality* (not teachable) — staff should be outgoing without being intrusive, friendly, active, energetic, and above all proactive.
- Signature philosophy line: **"Always simple | Never basic."** Meaning: focus on what matters, avoid ostentation. Example given: a Gin & Tonic — a highball, good gin, good tonic, clear ice, and a stirrer — "simple but not at all basic... this is true elegance, the rest is ostentation."
- "A complaint is a challenge to do our best, not a lost battle." No guest should ever leave with a bad experience.
- "The real host treats the worst customer like the woman in his life."

## Staff responsibilities (verbatim list)

- Maintain personal cleanliness, bar cleanliness, and personal spaces (locker/changing room)
- Check shifts regularly and be flexible when required
- Follow opening, closing and cleaning procedures as specified (in a separate "Bar Book")
- Interact with guests, be attentive and proactive to their needs
- Serve drinks always following house standards and recipes
- Be accountable for payments made
- Inform the manager of drops in standards, missing references, or complaints
- Follow rules on doses served and opening hours
- Do cleaning/bar-backing when needed
- Be fully responsible for one's section and aware of each guest's situation (who has had a drink, who is waiting, etc.)

## House standards & operations

**Uniform:** Not provided by the venue; staff buy their own, subject to manager approval. Comfortable but not scruffy; long-sleeved shirt recommended for men. Wash hands on arrival and use "amuchina" (hand sanitizer) every time money is touched if bartending.

**Punctuality:** Treated as the first sign of professionalism; lateness must be reported immediately to the shift manager.

**Dropbox:** Used to share menu, shifts, training docs, etc. — staff must register and share their email with the manager.

**Rota** (shift schedule): distributed in print and via Dropbox; days-off requests go through written communication (WhatsApp) to the manager.

**Breaks:** Dinner break ~45 minutes; evening breaks possible if coverage is arranged with a colleague. On returning from a cigarette break: wash hands and take a mint.

**Complaints:** Inform the manager immediately; the manager has authority to comp drinks etc. If the manager is absent, side with the guest and ensure no one leaves unsatisfied.

**Staff as guests:** 50% staff discount for the staff member plus two guests on days off; must follow the same rules as regular guests (don't clear tables, don't go behind the bar, don't self-serve).

**Music:** Controlled by the manager via tablet; staff can suggest playlist ideas. Staff may be asked to control the amplifier during live concerts.

**Age/behavior policy:** No minors under any circumstance. Overserved guests: stop serving them, discreetly and always ultimately at the manager's discretion (may be escorted out in exceptional cases). One customer in the bathroom at a time.

### Relations with guests (floor etiquette)

- Welcome guests confidently, make eye contact, smile, say good evening
- Engage solo guests at the counter — many prefer the bar precisely for the interaction; memorize preferences for return visits
- **Acknowledge any newly arrived guest within 60 seconds**, even when slammed, so they don't feel ignored
- When surrounded at the counter, take 2-3 orders simultaneously to find overlaps and speed service; suggest a round of beers/G&Ts if truly slammed
- Small but important attentions: change a wet coaster/towel, rearrange a table while guests are at the toilet/smoking, remove garnishes a drunk guest has discarded
- If a guest groans about a drink, ask what's wrong and fix it — don't let dissatisfaction go unaddressed
- Style of service: **polite but not formal, elegant but relaxed, friendly but not intrusive**
- Replace spilled drinks free of charge, no questions asked

### The 7-step service routine

1. **Welcome all guests within 30 seconds of arrival** — even a quick hello if busy
2. **Bring water while briefly explaining the menu** — mention any 86'd items, note classics are available; bring an English menu for non-Italian guests
3. **Take the order and write it down** — read the guest (how they speak, what they want), adapt vocabulary/register; don't be afraid to suggest premium spirits; never make a guest feel unsophisticated for a request — always offer alternatives instead of a flat "we don't have that"; crouch to eye level when guests are seated (attributed to Sasha Petraske); for large groups, repeat the order back; warn of >20 minute waits; **no drink is made without a written order**
4. **Serve drinks and do the checkback** — always with a coaster, changed each drink; no fixed serving order to the table; checkback should ask specifically ("how are your drinks? are they to your liking?") rather than generic "is everything okay?"; if a drink is disliked, replace it without discussion, with a smile, never make the guest feel judged; use the water refill as a natural excuse to return to the table
5. **Clear the table** — remove the empty glass with its coaster once finished, wait a few minutes then offer another drink; water bottle kept topped up
6. **Take payment** — verify all drinks are on the bill, present to the correct table, thank guests
7. **Farewell** — first and last impressions matter most; sincerely thank departing guests

### "What separates a poor job from a good job" (verbatim list)

- Always work at the same pace (80% of maximum = consistency + sufficient speed) on both slow and busy nights
- Always decorate the drink as intended
- Keep the bar top clean even on busy nights
- Always ask if guests want another drink
- Recognize regulars and use their names
- Communicate proactively when something is about to run out
- Never waste time — stay active even while chatting with guests
- Never touch the rim of glasses — hold from the lowest point
- Read guest needs in advance (e.g., offer to point out the smoking room if they stand without their jacket)
- Escort guests to services when possible
- Good service anticipates needs seconds in advance and is "invisible"

## Drink preparation standards

- Style: simple, aimed at showcasing quality raw ingredients, in line with "always simple, never basic"
- **All drinks are tasted with tasting straws and served with a metal straw**
- **Pour sizes:** Vermouth, aperitifs, liqueurs, amari = 75 ml unless otherwise specified; wine = 150 ml; Port and Sherry = 100 ml; all other spirits = 50 ml
- Recipes are written in mL
- **No free pouring or "throwing"** (flair/inconsistent techniques) — precision only
- Bottles always held label-facing the guest
- Preference for classics/twists over full "do-it-yourself" bespoke creations — "if these drinks have stood the test of time, there must be a reason"; any bespoke drink should use low-turnover stock
- All cocktails made by every bartender to the same specs/recipe/garnish for consistency
- Soda and beers poured in front of the guest
- Drinks waiting too long on the pass go into the freezer
- Ask for help rather than let service quality slip when overwhelmed; long orders split between two bartenders
- **Drink-making routine:** prep shaker and garnish first (garnish BEFORE making the drink); build order = Bitters → Juices → Modifiers (liqueurs, syrups) → Base spirit (cheapest ingredient first to minimize waste on a mistake); double shake/double stir standard; pour drinks starting with those served over ice; maximize two-handed multitasking during busy periods

## Reservations & floor management

- Reservations taken by phone/email: name, time, headcount, and (for live music nights) whether they want the music room. Reservation held 20 minutes past the booked time before reassignment.
- Marked at start of evening on the reservation sheet/agenda, unmarked on arrival, tracked with a special card.
- Appendix expands with granular rules: sofa/table combos on holidays (2-hour cap), large groups (10+) arrival/departure windows, staggering busy-day bookings, seating priority (best tables to inconvenienced regulars first on weekdays; fill to capacity on holidays), walk-ins seated on tables reserved 2+ hours out to generate "double shifts," releasing tables 15 minutes before the next booking to allow cleaning, confirming reservations tactfully before turning away queuing walk-ins, and placing a "reserved" card immediately when a table is about to turn over.

## Floor tips & tricks (appendix — "active vs. reactive work")

- Floor work is *reactive* (respond to unpredictable guest events); bar work is *active* (constant, even-paced production at 80% speed). The floor "filters" unpredictability so the bar can stay steady.
- **"Pockets of time":** moments where the guest can wait (a little) vs. moments where they cannot. Guest arrival and menu delivery cannot wait; water delivery and order-taking (up to 30s) can be slightly delayed; once an order is placed, the wait shifts to the bartender, freeing the server; once the bell rings, drink delivery is immediately top priority (perishable — put in freezer if excessively delayed).
- **Priority-level table** (guest arrival = 3, bring menu = 3, bring water = 1, take order = 2, deliver order = 3, take payment = 2, clear table w/ no one waiting = 1, clear table w/ someone waiting = 2, satisfy a colleague's request = 2).
- **"Never empty-handed":** always carry something useful in each direction between points (e.g., bring drinks to table 1, water to table 2, menu to table 3 on the way out; clear table 4 on the way back).
- **Pacing:** avoid batching two orders together if possible since ~30 seconds matters more to the bartender than to waiting guests; a floor lead can deliberately accelerate table ordering ahead of a known big table (e.g., 10:30pm) to get the bar "in rhythm" beforehand, or deliberately slow/dilute incoming orders if the bartender is already overwhelmed.

## Operational policies

- Staff alcohol only with manager authorization ("staff shot"); staff must finish closing duties and confirm they're not needed before sitting down; standard after-work drink is a beer or G&T.
- Smoking only on the fire escape, with coverage arranged and hands washed + mint afterward; no cigarette butts on the stairs.
- Staff must always be doing something — ask for a task if idle.
- Phone use during service minimized and kept out of guest sight.
- No take-away drinks served.
- No checks or foreign currency accepted as payment.
- No outside food allowed in the room.
- **Hours:** Tuesday–Saturday, 21:00–02:00. Closing sequence: 1:45 last call announced; 2:00 drink production stops, music/lights fade up gradually; 2:30 guests asked to finish drinks (if authorized by manager), then close-out checks performed.
- Dogs welcome on a leash.
- Staff represent the venue 24/7, even off-duty, including as guests of other bars.
- Emergency evacuation password: "Carlina."
- Local reference info: Mad Dog phone, taxi number, nearest station (Porta Nuova, 20 min walk), nearest taxi stand (Piazza Vittorio, 10 min), and lists of neighboring recommended wine bars, cocktail bars, daytime bars, and restaurants (all within ~15 minutes on foot) — used for guiding guests when the Mad Dog can't accommodate them or for staff recommendations.

## Closing statement

The manual explicitly disclaims being an exhaustive "vademecum" — its goal is to instill the "Mad Dog mentality" (values + working style) so that staff can use common sense in situations not directly covered. Final line: "Be professional, courteous, smile and enjoy doing it: after all, our industry is also the entertainment industry."

# Cocktail Codex: Fundamentals, Formulas, Evolutions

**Authors:** Alex Day, Nick Fauchald, and David Kaplan (founders of Death & Co / the 86 Co., with the Proprietors LLC bar group — Existing Conditions, the Normandie Club, the Walker Inn)
**Publisher:** Ten Speed Press / Penguin Random House, isbn13 9781607749707, 326 pages

## Core thesis and structural overview

The book's central argument: nearly every classic and modern cocktail is a variation on one of **six "root" cocktails**, each representing a distinct structural template. Rather than memorizing hundreds of recipes, a bartender who understands the six roots — their defining ratios, their "core" (primary flavor-driving ingredient), their "balance" (what tempers the core), and their "seasoning" (what accents it) — can build or deconstruct almost any cocktail. Each of the six chapters follows an identical anatomy:

1. **The Classic Recipe** — the traditional textbook version
2. **Our Root Recipe** — the authors' own tuned "ideal" version, with a stated rationale
3. **Orthodoxy** — a boxed statement of the template's defining traits
4. **Understanding the Template** — theory
5. **The Core** — deep dive on the primary spirit/ingredient category
6. **Experimenting with the Core** — riffs that swap the base
7. **The Balance** — deep dive on the tempering ingredient (sugar, vermouth, citrus, liqueur, seltzer, egg/dairy)
8. **Experimenting with the Balance** — riffs
9. **The Seasoning** — deep dive on the accent ingredient (bitters, garnish, acids, amaro)
10. **Experimenting with the Seasoning** — riffs
11. **[Chapter's] Extended Family** — a mind-map-style family tree connecting the root to dozens of named cocktails
12. **Exploring Technique** — a focused technical skill tied to that chapter (dilution, garnish, clarification, building, rimming, eggs/smoke)
13. **Glassware** — the chapter's signature glass

An **Appendix** (roughly the book's final 50 pages) collects the modern/original cocktail recipes referenced throughout (alphabetical, by contributing bartender and year) plus a technical reference section: syrups and cordials, infusions (including sous-vide/immersion-circulator and iSi-whipper/N2O rapid-infusion methods), house mixes and sodas, salts/rims, and acid/tincture solutions (with a full guide to clarifying juice via agar, centrifuge, or Pectinex/kieselsol-chitosan fining).

## The Six Root Cocktails

### 1. The Old-Fashioned — spirit, sugar, bitters

**Classic recipe:** 1 sugar cube, 2 dashes Angostura bitters, 2 oz bourbon, muddled/stirred with ice, lemon and orange twist.

**Root recipe (their ideal):**
> 2 oz Elijah Craig Small Batch bourbon, 1 teaspoon Demerara Gum Syrup, 2 dashes Angostura bitters, 1 dash Bitter Truth aromatic bitters, orange twist + lemon twist.

**Orthodoxy:** (1) spirit-driven; (2) balanced by a small amount of sweetness; (3) seasoned with bitters and a garnish.

- **The Core — American whiskey:** bourbon (≥51% corn; corn=sweetness, rye=spice, wheat=softness) vs. rye (≥51% rye, "flips" bourbon's mashbill, spicier). Recommended bourbons: Eagle Rare 10-Year, Elijah Craig Small Batch (their benchmark), Old Grand-Dad 114 (57% ABV, "sip slowly"), Old Weller Antique 107, Wyoming Whiskey. Recommended ryes: Rittenhouse (bonded, 100 proof), Russell's Reserve 6-Year. Also covers Tennessee sour-mash and wheat whiskey.
- **The Balance — sugar:** they reject muddling a sugar cube (dissolves poorly) and reject plain simple syrup (too thin) in favor of **Demerara Gum Syrup** — 2 parts demerara sugar to 1 part water, thickened with gum arabic — because it adds body/viscosity without diluting the spirit's character. Aged spirits (vanillin/oak-derived sweetness) may need less added sugar.
- **The Seasoning — bitters:** distinguishes "flavoring bitters" (add a distinct new flavor, e.g. Angostura aromatic) from "amplifying bitters" (heighten flavors already present, e.g. orange bitters). Categorizes bitters as aromatic, citrus, or savory (e.g. Bittermens Xocolatl mole bitters).
- **Technique — "underdiluted stirring":** because an Old-Fashioned continues diluting in the glass after serving, they deliberately under-dilute versus a Martini's full dilution — building it in a mixing glass with 1-inch ice cubes, stirring only to the point the spirit is still "sharp and slightly abrasive," then straining over one large cube to slow further dilution (~3 min traditional method vs. their faster technique).
- **Glassware:** the eponymous rocks glass, 12–14 oz capacity, thick weighted base, tapering slightly toward the base.
- **Extended Family (family tree):** Old-Fashioned variations split into "Experimenting with the Balance" (Fancy-Free, Monte Carlo, Chrysanthemum, Stinger — using liqueur instead of sugar), "Experimenting with the Seasoning" (Improved Whiskey Cocktail, Pop Quiz, Night Owl, Normandie Club Old-Fashioned), "Experimenting with the Core" (Vermouth Cocktail, Ti' Punch, Exit Strategy, Golden Boy), and further out, the whole extended family: **Champagne Cocktail, Mint Julep, Sherry Cobbler, Hot Toddy, and Sazerac** — each treated as an Old-Fashioned in disguise (Champagne Cocktail = spirit swapped for Champagne, no stirring needed since bubbles do the dilution).

### 2. The Martini — spirit and aromatized wine

**Root recipe (gin):** ~2 oz Plymouth gin, 1 oz Dolin dry vermouth, a dash of House Orange Bitters, lemon twist.
**Root recipe (vodka):** a drier split (roughly 2.5 oz Absolut Elyx vodka to 0.5 oz Dolin dry vermouth), olive garnish.

**Orthodoxy (paraphrased from chapter):** the Martini's core is defined by *two* ingredients in relationship — a base spirit and an aromatized/fortified wine — rather than one dominant spirit; vermouth is not a mere modifier but a co-equal partner.

- **The Core — gin:** distinguishes **London dry gin** (juniper-forward, all flavor from distillation, no post-distillation additives) from **contemporary/New Western gins** (juniper present but not dominant, showcase other botanicals — citrus, florals). Recommends Beefeater and Plymouth as versatile London-style; discusses genever and Old Tom as historical antecedents.
- **The Balance — vermouth:** treated with the same rigor as spirits — dry, sweet, and blanc vermouths each explored, plus fino/manzanilla sherry as a vermouth substitute in low-ABV Martini riffs.
- **Extended family and variations:** the Martini chapter's family includes the **Manhattan** (whiskey + sweet vermouth + bitters — essentially a Martini with the polarity of sweet/dry flipped and whiskey instead of gin), the **Vesper** (gin + vodka + Lillet Blanc), the **Negroni** (gin, sweet vermouth, Campari — equal-parts riff on the Martini template with an added bitter liqueur), the **Martinez** (an Old Tom gin/sweet vermouth/maraschino/bitters proto-Manhattan-Martini hybrid), and the **Bijou** (gin, green Chartreuse, sweet vermouth, orange bitters — reliant on a large dose of green Chartreuse balanced by Campari-like bitterness elsewhere in its lineage). Bottled aromatized wines (Lillet, Cocchi Americano, Dubonnet) are treated as legitimate cores/modifiers in their own right.
- Glassware discussion favors the coupe and the Nick & Nora for stirred, spirit-forward drinks.

### 3. The Daiquiri — spirit, citrus, sweetener (a "sour")

**Classic recipe:** 2 oz rum, ¾ oz fresh lime juice, ¾ oz simple syrup, shaken, strained up, lime wedge garnish.

**The universal "Basic Sour" formula** (stated explicitly as the template underlying not just Daiquiris but the whole sour category):
> **2 oz spirit : ¾ oz fresh lemon or lime juice : ¾ oz simple syrup** (shake with ice, strain, no garnish)

**Root recipe (their ideal Daiquiri):** 1¾ oz Caña Brava white rum, ¼ oz La Favorite Rhum Agricole Blanc (Coeur de Canne), 1 oz fresh lime juice, ¼ oz simple syrup, lime wedge.

**Orthodoxy:** (1) composed of a spirit, citrus, and a sweetener — typically rum, lime juice, and simple syrup; (2) flexible on the citrus:sweetener ratio depending on the drinker's preference and the citrus's actual acidity/sweetness; (3) requires improvisation because citrus juice is inherently inconsistent fruit-to-fruit.

- **The Core — rum:** categorized by production tradition — Spanish-style (ron, column-distilled, light, Cuba/Puerto Rico/Panama — recommends Caña Brava), English-style (pot-still, funkier, Jamaica), French-style (rhum agricole, made from fresh cane juice not molasses, grassy/vegetal), Jamaican rum. Explains why light/gold "filtered" rums can still be aged rums with color removed.
- **Distinguishes sours from daisies:** a **sour** (Daiquiri, Whiskey Sour) uses only spirit + citrus + sugar; a **daisy** (Margarita, Cosmopolitan) substitutes a sweetened liqueur (orange curaçao/Cointreau) for some of the sugar, making the liqueur part of the core rather than mere seasoning — this distinction is formalized fully in the Sidecar chapter.
- **Extended family (family tree, page 143):** the Daiquiri's family branches into Collins-style drinks (**Tom Collins**: 2 oz gin, ¾ oz lemon, ¾ oz simple syrup, topped with seltzer — a "sour with bubbles added," short-shaken 5 seconds to preserve carbonation), **Grapefruit Collins, Parachute, Moscow Mule, Dark and Stormy, Presbyterian** (all "spirit + acid + sweetener + seltzer" riffs), fizzes (**Silver Fizz** — Collins + egg white; **Ramos Gin Fizz** — Silver Fizz + heavy cream, 5–10 min shake), and Champagne/sparkling-wine sours (**Old Cuban** — Audrey Saunders' Mojito-with-Champagne; **French 75** — gin Collins topped with Champagne instead of seltzer, with the sour's proportions cut to accommodate the wine's own sugar/acid/alcohol).
- **Technique — juice clarification:** detailed methods for clarifying citrus/other juices with agar (gel-and-strain method), a centrifuge with Pectinex/kieselsol/chitosan fining agents, or simple fine-mesh straining — used to build clean-looking "clarified" cocktails like the Clarified Cosmopolitan.

### 4. The Sidecar — spirit, citrus, and a substantial liqueur (a "daisy")

**Classic recipe:** 1½ oz Cognac, 1 oz Cointreau, ¾ oz fresh lemon juice, orange twist.

**Root recipe (their ideal):** 1½ oz Pierre Ferrand Ambre Cognac, 1 oz Pierre Ferrand dry curaçao, ¾ oz fresh lemon juice, orange twist. (Traced to a likely predecessor, the 1862 Brandy Crusta.)

**Orthodoxy:** (1) core flavor = a spirit plus a substantial amount of flavorful liqueur (generally ½–1 oz); (2) balanced *and* seasoned by that liqueur (which contributes sweetness, sometimes alongside another sweetener); (3) also balanced by highly acidic citrus (lemon or lime).

- **Key distinction from the Daiquiri:** liqueurs vary enormously in proof and sweetness by producer (dry Pierre Ferrand curaçao vs. sweet Grand Marnier vs. middling Cointreau), so there is no single fixed template ratio — each liqueur must be tasted and the proportions rebalanced around it.
- **Liqueur reference (extensive):** orange curaçao, stone-fruit liqueurs (apricot, peach — Massenez Crème de Pêche), pear liqueurs (Clear Creek, Mathilde), maraschino liqueur (Luxardo vs. sweeter Maraska), floral/herbal liqueurs (elderflower/St-Germain — "bartender's ketchup," violet liqueur/crème de violette, crème de menthe, Bénédictine, Chartreuse yellow vs. green, Galliano, Strega), and rich liqueurs (crème de cacao light vs. dark, vanilla liqueur, coffee liqueur).
- **Variations by liqueur swap:** **20th Century** (gin, Lillet blanc, white crème de cacao, lemon — "chocolaty but not dessert"), **Last Word** (equal parts gin, green Chartreuse, maraschino, lime — a Sidecar template pushed to high-proof extremes), **Crop Top** (amaro-based lighter riff on the Last Word), **Why Not** (a Whiskey Sour/Sidecar hybrid using maple syrup for half the Cointreau), **Cosmopolitan** and its **Clarified Cosmopolitan** variant (explicitly identified as "a Sidecar that swaps citrus vodka for Cognac and lime juice for lemon, with cranberry added" — and a caution against overusing sweetened cranberry cocktail mix), **Lily Pad** and **Pegu Cub Cocktail**.
- **Technique — acids:** an extensive reference on alternative/powdered acids used to manipulate or replace citrus — **citric acid** (the primary acid in lemon/lime, made into a 100g water:25g powder solution), **malic acid** (green-apple tartness, weaker so higher concentration), **tartaric acid** (found in grapes/wine, drying), **lactic acid** (creamy texture without dairy), **ascorbic acid/vitamin C** (antioxidant, prevents juice browning), and **phosphoric acid** (Coca-Cola's tartness, tongue-tingling without flavor). Also a full section on glass-rimming technique (skinny vs. fat rims, wetting only the outside, salt/sugar/spice rim recipes).
- **Glassware:** the coupe, upsized to ~7 oz (Retro Coupe Glass by Urban Bar) specifically to leave room for a shaken drink's foamy head without overflow.

### 5. The Whisky Highball — spirit and a nonalcoholic (often carbonated) mixer

**Classic recipe:** 2 oz scotch, 6 oz cold seltzer, lemon wedge.

**Root recipe (their ideal):** 2 oz Hakushu 12-Year Japanese whisky, 4 oz cold seltzer, no garnish (garnish is deliberately omitted to let the whisky/water pairing stand unmasked).

**Orthodoxy:** (1) core spirit that also provides seasoning; (2) core can be split between any number of spirits/wines/fortified wines; (3) can be effervescent or still.

- **The Core & Seasoning — whisky:** full survey of **Scotch** (single-malt vs. blended; regions — Highlands/Speyside, Lowlands, Islay; recommends Auchentoshan Three Wood, Bowmore 12, Glenlivet 12, Highland Park 12, Laphroaig 10, and blends The Famous Grouse / Compass Box Asyla/Oak Cross), **Irish whiskey** (triple-distilled, larger stills, blends like Bushmills vs. pure pot-still like Redbreast 12), and **Japanese whisky** (Hakushu, Nikka Coffey Grain, Suntory Toki) — with a strategy for mixing with expensive single-malts (use a cheap blend as base, dose in a splash of the fine single-malt as an accent, rather than mixing the good stuff wholesale).
- **The Balance — sparkling water:** distinguishes **sparkling mineral water** (variable mineral content/effervescence), **soda water/club soda** (minerals added deliberately, subtle salinity that brightens citrus and curbs bitterness), and **seltzer** (neutral, additive-free, their default for control). Two-parts-mixer-to-one-part-whisky is their guideline; more assertive whiskies may need more seltzer.
- **Technique — "Building Whisky Highballs":** a precise Japanese-bar-inspired build: chill the glass, add one large (1-inch+) ice cube directly from the freezer, let temper ~10 seconds before stirring (to avoid cracking), stir until condensation forms (~10 sec), add ice to two-thirds full, pour chilled seltzer to just below flotation point, insert a barspoon to the bottom and gently lift/lower the lowest ice cube to mix without killing bubbles, add a final ice cube and one gentle full stir. A sidebar profiles Tyson Buhler (Death & Co bar director) describing Japan's *mizuwari* ritual (stir exactly 13.5 times clockwise after ice, then 3.5 times after water).
- **Extended Family (family tree):** two main experimentation branches — "Experimenting with the Core" (**Americano** — Campari + sweet vermouth + seltzer, essentially a Negroni-as-Highball) and "Experimenting with the Balance" (**Paloma, Cuba Libre, Screwdriver, Negroni Sbagliato** — swapping seltzer for tonic, cola, orange juice, or prosecco) — plus a further-out extended family: **Bellini, Mimosa, Kir Royale, Aperol Spritz** family, **Gin & Tonic, Tequila Sunrise, Harvey Wallbanger, Nobody's Robots, Apple Pop**, and savory Highballs like the **Bloody Mary** (and Bloody Mary Verde). Also covers the classic **Cuba Libre** (rum + lime + cola — lime peel oils drawn out to complement the cola's own citrus).
- **Glassware:** the Highball glass, ~12 oz, thick weighted base, thin etched rim (they note it is NOT interchangeable with the larger 14 oz Collins glass, which needs the extra volume for a fully-diluted shaken drink).

### 6. The Flip — spirit/fortified wine, sugar, and whole egg

**Classic recipe:** 2 oz spirit or fortified wine, 2 teaspoons demerara sugar, 1 whole egg, dry-shaken then shaken with ice, double-strained, nutmeg garnish. (Traced to 17th-century England — beer/rum/sugar heated with a red-hot iron "poker" that made it "flip"/froth; egg and cold service came later.)

**Root recipe (their ideal):** 2 oz González Byass Matusalem oloroso sherry, ½ oz Demerara Gum Syrup, 1 whole egg, nutmeg — into a small white wine glass.

**Orthodoxy:** (1) characteristic flavor from a core spirit or fortified wine plus a rich ingredient; (2) balanced by rich ingredients — eggs, dairy, coconut milk, or dense liqueurs/syrups; (3) seasoned with aromatic spices dusted on top (or a flavorful amaro).

- **The Core — fortified wine (sherry/port/madeira):** an extensive reference — **sherry** styles from bone-dry **fino/manzanilla** (flor-aged, briny) through **amontillado** (biological + oxidative aging, nutty) and **palo cortado** (an amontillado that "escaped" its flor early, smells like oloroso/tastes like amontillado) to rich **oloroso** (fully oxidative, fig/raisin, likened to sweet vermouth without the botanicals) and sweet **Pedro Ximénez/Moscatel** ("nature's finest simple syrup"); explains the **solera** fractional-blending aging system. **Port** styles: ruby, vintage, late-bottled vintage, tawny, colheita. **Madeira**: rainwater (light) through richer aged styles.
- **The Balance — eggs and dairy:** whole egg gives both fatty body (yolk) and foam (white); egg yolk alone (rare) adds richer flavor via lecithin's emulsifying power (used in the **New York Flip** — bourbon, tawny port, simple syrup, heavy cream, egg yolk — a decadent version of the lemon-based New York Sour). Dairy section strongly prefers **heavy cream** (35–40% fat, less prone to curdling with acid, carries flavor) over half-and-half or whole milk, plus nut-milk alternatives (their own **almond milk**: 600 g sliced blanched almonds blended with 7½ cups water, strained). Both eggs and dairy need a slightly longer shake and double-straining for smooth texture.
- **The Seasoning — amaro:** classified by sweetness/bitterness into **aperitivi** (lightest, e.g. Aperol at 11% ABV), light, medium, and dense amari — with no fixed rules, since amaro can play anywhere from background seasoning to full core spirit.
- **Rich-drink family:** the Flip's "strike it rich" extended family explicitly includes the **White Russian, Grasshopper, Brandy Alexander** (crème de cacao + cream, no egg — "Flip without the egg"), **Coffee Cocktail** (tawny port + Cognac + egg, named for its coffee-cup *appearance*, not flavor), **Eggnog** (single-serving fast version: rum, Cognac, vanilla liqueur, cane sugar syrup, heavy cream, whole egg), and **Tom and Jerry** (a full egg/sugar/spice batter recipe given in the appendix).
- **Technique — next-level smoking:** how to infuse cocktails/ingredients with real smoke using a smoking gun (e.g. PolyScience Smoking Gun Pro), wood staves/planks, torches, or grilling — with a wood-selection guide from mild (cherry, apple, birch) through medium (oak, hickory — their top all-purpose picks) to strong (mesquite, walnut), plus smoking with flammable ingredients (torched rosemary, smoked tea like Lapsang souchong, and smoke-infused ice).

## Appendix highlights (technical reference)

- **Signature original cocktails** from the authors' bars (Existing Conditions, Normandie Club, Death & Co, the Walker Inn) with full specs, e.g. **Penicillin** (Sam Ross, 2005): 2 oz blended Scotch, ¾ oz lemon, ⅓ oz honey syrup, ⅓ oz House Ginger Syrup, topped with a mist of Islay scotch and candied ginger.
- **Syrups/cordials:** Basil Stem Syrup, Grilled Pineapple Syrup, House Lemon Cordial, House Orgeat (almond milk + sugar + Cognac + amaretto + rose water), Meyer Lemon Cordial (sous-vide, immersion circulator at 130°F), Raisin Honey Syrup, Strawberry Cream Syrup, Vanilla Lactic Syrup, White Honey Syrup.
- **Infusions:** dozens of spirit infusions using either an immersion circulator (sous-vide, typically 140–145°F for 2 hours) or an iSi whipper with N2O cartridges for rapid infusion (charge, shake, vent, repeat) — e.g. Hickory Smoke-Infused Cognac, Cacao Nib-Infused Tequila/Ramazzotti/Pear Brandy, Chamomile-Infused Blanco Tequila/Calvados/Dolin Blanc/Cocchi Americano/Rye, Coconut-Infused Bourbon, Jalapeño-Infused Vodka, Madras Curry-Infused Gin, Oolong-Infused Vodka, Raisin-Infused Rye/Scotch, Sesame-Infused Rum, Milk-Washed Rum (a clarification technique using citric acid to curdle milk, then filter).
- **House mixes/sodas:** Apple Celery Soda, Basic Bloody Mary Mix, Donn's Mix No. 1 (tiki syrup: grapefruit juice + Cinnamon Syrup), Homemade Grapefruit Soda, House Coconut Cream, House Orange Bitters (blend of three commercial bitters), Milk & Honey House Curaçao, Normandie Club Bloody Mary Mix and House Sweet Vermouth, Tom and Jerry Batter, Verde Mix.
- **Salts, rims, solutions, tinctures:** Lemon Pepper Salt, Sugared Smoked Salt, Après-Ski Tincture (vodka + cedar incense scrapings + cinnamon), Cacao Nib Tincture, and the full citric/malic/tartaric/lactic/ascorbic/phosphoric acid solution recipes referenced throughout the book.

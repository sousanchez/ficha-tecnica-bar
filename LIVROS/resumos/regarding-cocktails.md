# Regarding Cocktails

**Author:** Sasha Petraske, with Georgette Moger-Petraske
**Forewords:** Dale DeGroff, Robert Simonson
**Publisher:** Phaidon Press, ISBN (Phaidon), hardcover
**Source file:** `Regarding Cocktails (Sasha Petraske, Georgette Moger-Petraske etc.) (z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk).pdf` (264 pages, scanned/photographed edition — no text layer; content extracted via page-image transcription)

Sasha Petraske (1973–2015) founded Milk & Honey on Eldridge Street in 2000, widely credited with launching the modern "speakeasy" craft-cocktail revival, and went on to open Little Branch, Dutch Kills, Middle Branch, Lantern's Keep, Milk & Honey London, and other bars, mentoring a generation of bartenders (Sam Ross, Richard Boccato, Michael Madrusan, Toby Maloney, Chad Solomon, Theo Lieberman, Joseph Schwartz, Eric Alperin, and more). He died before finishing this book; his widow Georgette Moger-Petraske completed it posthumously, assembling his essays, house rules, and recipes alongside first-person recollections from the "Milk & Honey family" of bartenders he trained. Each recipe in the book is presented under a named contributor's byline with a short personal anecdote about Sasha, followed by the exact spec.

## Philosophy and Technique Synthesis

**The five-drink taxonomy.** Sasha's core teaching organizes essentially all cocktails into five families — Old Fashioned, Martini/Manhattan, Sour, Highball, Fix — a structure the book itself adopts as its table of contents. Learning to build within these five templates, rather than memorizing individual recipes, was his method for training new bartenders quickly.

**Precision and measurement.** Every recipe uses exact jigger measurements (down to ⅛ oz / ~4 ml), and Sasha was known for obsessive iteration — Matt Clark recounts spending "an hour and a half adjusting [The Sherpa] incrementally in different directions" only to land back on the first version tried. The lesson: exactness in measurement is what separates a "right" drink from a merely close one, because ingredient variation (a lime's acidity, a peach's ripeness) means even a "correct" recipe needs to be tasted and adjusted every single time.

**Ice and dilution (Richard Boccato's essay, quoting Sasha directly).** Key principles:
- Ice must be *dry* before use — most cooling energy transfers at the phase change (ice→water); wet ice adds water on top of ice, throwing off the intended dilution.
- Shake with large-format ice for a longer shake time (more chilling, better ice-crystal "head" on a strained drink) — the big cube must eventually break apart during the shake, or the drink stays underchilled ("thukka thukka thukka" = bad).
- A frozen spirit + 4 Kold-Draft cubes shaken briefly can replicate a fully-diluted Daiquiri.
- Target serving temperature: roughly ‑4°C (25°F), not the unrealistic ‑8°C often cited — every glass shape/size has its own "sweet spot" temperature that holds for about 20 minutes.
- Reject "dilution" as a vague term; think instead in final **ABV%** — total alcohol volume ÷ finished liquid volume. Example given: a shaken Daiquiri with 2 oz (60 ml) 80-proof rum, ~3.75 oz pre-shake volume, and 4.5 oz post-shake volume works out to 0.8 ÷ 4.5 = ~17% ABV, not the commonly cited "25–30% water from ice" rule of thumb.
- Drinks should be built/shaken/stirred to hit a specific **washline** (the gap left between liquid and rim) appropriate to the glass, never poured to the brim, and never with residual liquid left behind in the shaker/mixing glass ("always good to the last drop").

**Assembly order and jiggering (from Dale DeGroff's foreword, describing Sasha's early self-training):** build sour ingredient → sweet ingredient → dashes/splashes → base spirit → ice, always with a jigger (never free-poured), and always with all-metal Boston shaker sets (cited as Sasha's innovation that changed the entire bar-tool industry toward metal-on-metal shakers).

**Garnish philosophy ("Consider the Peacock").** Distinguishes *garnish-as-ingredient* (an Old Fashioned's citrus oils, a Julep's mint — essential, never omit) from *pure ornament* (candied ginger, a raspberry on a Fix) — for the latter, poor quality or wilted presentation is read as a warning sign that other corners are being cut, but a bartender should skip or substitute an ornamental garnish rather than serve a shoddy one; "it is better to garnish a Mojito with a lime wedge than with a sad sprig of mint."

**House Rules (from Milk & Honey, cast in bronze on the bathroom doors of Sasha's bars):**
1. No name-dropping, no star f*cking.
2. No hooting, hollering, shouting, or other loud behavior.
3. No fighting, no play fighting, no talking about fighting.
4. Gentlemen will remove their hats. Hooks are provided.
5. Gentlemen will not introduce themselves to ladies. Ladies, feel free to start a conversation or ask the bartender to introduce you. If a man you don't know speaks to you, please lift your chin slightly and ignore him.
6. Do not linger outside the front door.
7. Do not bring anyone unless you would leave that person alone in your home. You are responsible for the behavior of your guests.
8. Exit the bar briskly and silently. People are trying to sleep across the street. Please make all your travel plans and say all farewells before leaving the bar.

**On personal conduct.** Sasha's own maxims, quoted throughout the Guides section, include "Being early is not a waste of time," "Eat before you are starving," and "Remember things always take longer than you think they will." He held any piece of glassware at its lowest point, insisted bartenders wash their hands the instant they touched their face or hair during service, and co-founded the not-for-profit San Antonio Cocktail Conference (with Scott Becker), which channeled 100% of profits to children's charities (HeartGift, ChildSafe, Transplants for Children, The Children's Shelter) under the slogan "Pouring Our Hearts Out."

## Component Library (referenced throughout every chapter)

- **Simple Syrup** — 1:2 sugar:water by volume (½ cup/100 g sugar : ½ cup/120 ml water), never boiled (boiling evaporates water and skews the ratio)
- **Violette Syrup** — 2 oz gin : 1 oz violet syrup (e.g. Monin) : 1 oz Simple Syrup
- **Ginger Syrup** — equal parts fresh ginger juice and superfine sugar
- **Honey Syrup** — 1 cup honey : ⅓ cup hot water
- **Orgeat Syrup** — toasted slivered almonds, sugar, water, cooked into a syrup, then finished with vodka and orange flower water, strained through a jelly bag
- **Mineral Saline** — ½ cup kosher salt dissolved in 4½ cups warmed mineral water
- **A.P.P. Bitters** (house blend) — equal parts Angostura, Peychaud's, and Dale DeGroff's Pimento bitters
- **House Orange Bitters** — equal parts Fee Brothers West Indian Orange bitters and Regan's No. 6
- **White Peach Purée** — 1 white peach (skin on) blended with 1 oz fresh Meyer lemon juice and 1 oz Simple Syrup, strained

## Recipe Sample — Organized by Chapter

### The Old Fashioned

**American Trilogy** (whiskey glass) — 1 small brown sugar cube saturated with 2–4 dashes orange bitters, muddled with a bar-spoon splash of club soda into a paste; add 1 oz (30 ml) rye whiskey, 1 oz (30 ml) bonded applejack, and 1 large ice cube. Stir 10–15 times. Garnish with an orange twist.

**Home on the Range** (rocks glass) — Build over 1 large ice cube: 3 dashes Angostura bitters, ¼ oz (7.5 ml) sweet vermouth (preferably Cocchi Vermouth di Torino), ¼ oz (7.5 ml) Cointreau, 2 oz (60 ml) bourbon. Stir a few times. Garnish with a lemon twist and an orange twist.

**The Sherpa** (whiskey glass) — 2 dashes orange bitters, ¼ oz (7.5 ml) Curaçao, ¼ oz (7.5 ml) St. Elizabeth Allspice Dram, 2 oz (60 ml) bourbon (preferably Elijah Craig 12-year), built over 1 large ice cube, stirred a few times. Garnish with a lemon twist. (Designed as a slow-sipping drink, kept low on water content.)

**Si-Güey** (whiskey glass) — Build over 1 large ice cube: 3 dashes orange bitters, ¼ oz (7.5 ml) Curaçao, 2 oz (60 ml) tequila reposado. Stir, then finish with a ¼ oz (7.5 ml) float of Islay whisky.

**Tattletale** (room-temperature whiskey glass) — 3 dashes Angostura bitters, 1 bar spoon honey, 1¼ oz (37.5 ml) Highland Scotch, ¾ oz (22 ml) Islay whisky, built over 1 rock ice cube and stirred 5–6 times. Garnish with lemon and orange twists. (A smoky riff on an Old Fashioned, using honey instead of sugar — created by Sam Ross for Sasha, who hated smoky Scotch but loved this.)

**.38 Special** (coupe) — 2⅜ oz (67.5 ml) blended Scotch, ⅜ oz (11 ml) yellow Chartreuse, ⅜ oz (11 ml) Amaro CioCiaro, stirred with ice and strained. Garnish with a lemon twist.

### The Martini and the Manhattan

**Martini** — 1 oz (30 ml) dry vermouth, 2 oz (60 ml) gin (a wet 2:1 ratio — Sasha's house Martini was notably wetter than convention), stirred in a frozen mixing glass filled with ice, strained into a chilled coupe. Garnish with an olive, cocktail onion, or lemon twist. (Petraske-house stirred drinks are built to a 3-oz total liquid volume.)

**Red Hook** — 2 oz (60 ml) rye whiskey, ½ oz (15 ml) Maraschino liqueur (preferably Luxardo), ½ oz (15 ml) Punt e Mes vermouth, stirred with ice, strained into a chilled coupe. (Created by Vincenzo Errico at Milk & Honey in 2003 — the first of the "Brooklyn neighborhood" Manhattan variations.)

**Bensonhurst** — 1 oz (30 ml) dry vermouth (preferably Dolin), 1 tsp (5 ml) Cynar, 2 tsp (10 ml) Maraschino liqueur (preferably Luxardo), 2 oz (60 ml) rye whiskey (preferably Rittenhouse), stirred in a frozen mixing glass, strained into a frozen coupe.

**Cobble Hill** — 3 thin cucumber slices gently muddled in a mixing glass; add 2 oz (60 ml) rye whiskey, ½ oz (15 ml) Amaro Montenegro, ½ oz (15 ml) dry vermouth, fill with ice, stir 30 seconds, strain into a chilled coupe. Garnish with the remaining cucumber slice.

**Deep Blue Sea** — 2 oz (60 ml) gin, ¾ oz (22 ml) Cocchi Americano, ¼ oz (7.5 ml) Violette Syrup, 2 dashes orange bitters, stirred with ice, strained into a frozen coupe. Garnish with a lemon twist.

**Fallback Cocktail** — 2 dashes Peychaud's bitters, ½ oz (15 ml) Amaro Nonino, ½ oz (15 ml) Carpano Antica, 1 oz (30 ml) applejack, 1 oz (30 ml) rye whiskey, built over 1 large ice cube in a whiskey glass and stirred. Garnish with an orange twist.

**Gin & It** — 2 oz (60 ml) gin, 1 oz (30 ml) sweet vermouth, stirred with ice, strained into a chilled coupe, finished with a lemon twist (Sasha and Georgette drank this at a strict 2:1 ratio and served it as a wedding favor batched in Mason jars).

**Left Hand** — 1½ oz (45 ml) bourbon, ¾ oz (22 ml) sweet vermouth, ¾ oz (22 ml) Campari, 3 dashes Bittermen's chocolate bitters, stirred 30 seconds with ice, strained into a chilled coupe. Garnish with a brandied cherry. (A bourbon-based Negroni riff predating the Boulevardier's revival.)

**Savoir Faire** — ¼ oz (7.5 ml) Avèze, ¼ oz (7.5 ml) dry vermouth (preferably Dolin), 2 oz (60 ml) gin, stirred with cracked ice in a chilled mixing glass; strain into a coupe lightly sprayed with orange blossom water; finish with a lemon twist rubbed on the rim then discarded.

**Spring Forward** — 2 oz (60 ml) gin, 1 oz (30 ml) dry vermouth, 2 spring onions or ramps (trimmed) — muddle 1 onion briefly with the gin/vermouth in a frozen mixing glass (overmuddling turns it bitter), fill with ice, stir, strain into a chilled coupe. Garnish with the remaining onion.

### The Sour

**Daiquiri** — ⅞ oz to 1 oz (26–30 ml) fresh lime juice (to taste — Sasha often used ⅞ oz since a full ounce can run too tart depending on the fruit), ¾ oz (22 ml) Simple Syrup, 2 oz (60 ml) white rum, shaken hard with a 2-inch ice cube, strained into a chilled coupe. (Called by Abraham Hawkins "the test drink for anyone who wants to see what a bar or bartender is all about.")

**Bee's Knees** — 2 oz (60 ml) gin, 1 oz (30 ml) fresh lemon juice, ¾ oz (22 ml) Honey Syrup, shaken with ice, strained into a chilled coupe. (Prohibition-era drink; honey and lemon originally masked bathtub gin.)

**The Business** — 2 oz (60 ml) gin, 1 oz (30 ml) fresh lime juice, ¾ oz (22 ml) Honey Syrup, shaken with 1 large ice cube, strained into a chilled coupe. (A wordplay variant of the Bee's Knees — say the two names back to back.)

**Apple Jack** — ½ oz (15 ml) fresh lemon juice, ½ oz (15 ml) Simple Syrup, ½ oz (15 ml) apple cider, 1½ oz (45 ml) applejack, shaken vigorously with ice, strained into a chilled coupe.

**Caipirinha** — 6 lime wedges, 2 oz (60 ml) cachaça, ¾ oz (22 ml) Simple Syrup, 1 white sugar cube, all muddled in a shaker, filled with cracked ice and shaken, strained into a chilled double rocks glass over fresh cracked ice.

**Calvados 75** — 1 oz (30 ml) Calvados or applejack, ½ oz (15 ml) fresh lemon juice, ½ oz (15 ml) Simple Syrup, shaken with cracked ice, strained into a Collins glass half-filled with cracked ice, topped with Champagne or cava.

**Champs-Élysées** — 2 oz (60 ml) Cognac, ¾ oz (22 ml) fresh lemon juice, ½ oz (15 ml) green Chartreuse, ¼ oz (7.5 ml) Simple Syrup, shaken with ice, strained into a chilled coupe. Garnish with a lemon twist. (A Sidecar riff swapping orange Curaçao for green Chartreuse.)

**Cosmonaut** — 2 oz (60 ml) gin, ¾ oz (22 ml) fresh lemon juice, 1 heaping bar spoon raspberry preserves, shaken with ice, strained into a chilled coupe. (Named as a jab at the Cosmopolitan's popularity; a variation on the 1930s Marmalade Cocktail.)

**Turnpike** — ¾ oz (22 ml) applejack, ¾ oz (22 ml) fresh lemon juice, ¾ oz (22 ml) Simple Syrup, 1 oz (30 ml) rye whiskey (preferably Monongahela), shaken with ice, strained into a chilled coupe. Garnish with a lemon wedge.

**Water Lily** — ¾ oz (22 ml) orange liqueur (Cointreau), ¾ oz (22 ml) Violette Syrup, ¾ oz (22 ml) fresh lemon juice, ¾ oz (22 ml) gin, shaken with ice, strained into a coupe. Garnish with a lemon twist. (Created for Georgette; named for her middle name, Lillian.)

### The Highball

**Gin & Tonic** — 2 oz (60 ml) gin poured over 1 large ice cube in a double Old Fashioned glass, served with a full 6 oz (180 ml) bottle of tonic water on the side and a lime wedge, so the guest controls dilution.

**Grapefruit Collins** — 2 oz (60 ml) gin, ¾ oz (22 ml) fresh lemon juice, ¾ oz (22 ml) Simple Syrup shaken with ice, strained into a Collins glass over 1 large Collins ice cube, topped with 1 oz (30 ml) fresh grapefruit juice and ½ oz (15 ml) club soda. Garnish with a grapefruit twist.

**Bicycle Thief** — 1 oz (30 ml) gin, 1 oz (30 ml) Campari, 1½ oz (45 ml) fresh grapefruit juice, ½ oz (15 ml) fresh lemon juice, ½ oz (15 ml) Simple Syrup, built in a highball glass over a long Collins cube or 3 medium ice cubes, topped with club soda. Garnish with a thin orange slice. (A Tarzan Cocktail variant swaps in fresh pineapple juice for the grapefruit.)

**Bottlerocket** — 2 oz (60 ml) tequila blanco, ¾ oz (22 ml) fresh lime juice, ¾ oz (22 ml) Honey Syrup, one ¼-inch (5 mm) jalapeño slice, shaken with ice, strained into a Collins glass over ice and topped with club soda.

**French 75** — 1 oz (30 ml) Cognac (or gin, "if requested"), ½ oz (15 ml) fresh lemon juice, ½ oz (15 ml) Simple Syrup, shaken with a small piece of cracked ice, strained into the small half of the shaker and topped with Champagne directly in the shaker, then "rolled" into a chilled flute or coupe. Garnish with a lemon twist.

**Hayes Fizz** — ¾ oz (22 ml) fresh lemon juice, ¾ oz (22 ml) Simple Syrup, 2 oz (60 ml) gin, shaken with 1 large ice cube; a Collins glass is rinsed with absinthe/Pernod, filled with a Collins ice cube, and the shaken drink is strained in, topped with 2 oz (60 ml) club soda. Garnish with an orange slice and a cocktail cherry. (Named for regular Reed Hayes, who added his own absinthe "offerings" to his Tom Collins.)

**Tritter Collins** — 2 oz (60 ml) gin, ¾ oz (22 ml) fresh lemon juice, ¾ oz (22 ml) Simple Syrup, ¼ oz (7.5 ml) absinthe, shaken with ice, strained into a Collins glass over a long ice cube, topped with 1 oz (30 ml) grapefruit juice and ½ oz (15 ml) club soda. Garnish with a grapefruit twist. (A Grapefruit Collins plus a dash of absinthe.)

**Tritter Rickey** — 2 oz (60 ml) gin, 1 oz (30 ml) fresh lime juice, ¾ oz (22 ml) Simple Syrup, shaken with ice; a Collins glass is coated with an absinthe rinse and filled with ice, the drink strained in, topped with club soda. Garnish with a fresh mint sprig.

### The Fix (Cobblers, Swizzles, Peasant Cocktails)

**Mojito** — 8–10 fresh mint leaves, 1 oz (30 ml) fresh lime juice, ¾ oz (22 ml) Simple Syrup, 1 brown sugar cube, gently muddled in a shaker; add 2 oz (60 ml) white rum, pour into a chilled rocks glass, top with crushed ice. Garnish with a mint bouquet. (Adapted from a 1931 Sloppy Joe's Bar, Havana recipe; the book traces its lineage back to "El Draque," a 16th-century precursor named for Sir Francis Drake.)

**Eskimo's Kiss** (double Mojito) — 10–12 fresh mint leaves, 2 Demerara sugar cubes, 2 oz (60 ml) fresh lime juice, 1½ oz (45 ml) Simple Syrup muddled together, then 4 oz (120 ml) white rum added and swirled in; poured into an imperial pint glass filled with crushed ice, garnished with a mint bouquet, served with two straws. (Sasha's own personal drink of choice, always doubled.)

**Queen's Park Swizzle (Dark)** — 8–10 fresh mint leaves, ¾ oz (22 ml) Demerara Simple Syrup, 1 oz (30 ml) fresh lime juice, gently muddled; add 2 oz (60 ml) overproof (80-proof) Demerara rum, transfer to a chilled Collins glass with mint settled at the bottom, fill ¾ with crushed ice and swizzle, add 4–6 dashes Angostura bitters, swizzle again for a tricolor effect, top with more crushed ice. Garnish with a mint bouquet.

**Queen's Park Swizzle (Light)** — Same build, but 1 brown sugar cube instead of Demerara syrup base measure adjustments, 2 oz (30 ml as printed — likely 2 oz) white rum (preferably Flor de Caña 4-Year), and 4–5 dashes Peychaud's bitters instead of Angostura for a red (rather than dark) float.

**Maloney Park Swizzle** — Same swizzle technique with 10–12 mint leaves, ¾ oz (22 ml) lime juice, ¾ oz (22 ml) Simple Syrup, 2 oz (60 ml) rum (preferably Matusalem Classico), and 5–6 dashes Peychaud's bitters swizzled to a "tricolor" float. (Named after Toby Maloney by Sasha.)

**Asbury Park Swizzle** — 3 mint sprigs, ¾ oz (22 ml) fresh lemon juice, ¾ oz (22 ml) Honey Syrup, ¼ oz (7.5 ml) apple liqueur (Berentzen's), 2 oz (60 ml) white applejack (Laird's Jersey Lightning), 2 drops Mineral Saline, 4–5 dashes A.P.P. Bitters, 1 dash House Orange Bitters — 2 mint sprigs muddled with lemon/honey/apple liqueur, applejack and saline swirled in, poured into a Collins glass with crushed ice, bitters swizzled into a red top layer, more ice added in a cone. (A Queen's-Park-Swizzle riff built around New Jersey's Laird's applejack, honoring Bruce Springsteen's *Greetings from Asbury Park*.)

**Bin & Gitters** — ¾ oz (22 ml) Simple Syrup, 1 oz (30 ml) fresh lime juice, 2 oz (60 ml) gin, shaken and strained into a chilled double rocks glass over crushed ice (¾ up the glass), with a dash of Angostura bitters swizzled in to create a visible color layer; topped with more crushed ice in a cone, served with a straw, no garnish ("no garnish for a bartender"). Effectively "a gimlet on crushed ice with a bitters float," per Sasha's own description.

**Strawberry Fix** — 1–2 hulled, quartered strawberries muddled gently in a chilled double rocks glass to form a "jam" layer; glass filled with crushed ice. Separately shake ¾ oz (22 ml) fresh lemon juice, ¾ oz (22 ml) Simple Syrup, 2 oz (60 ml) bourbon, and pour over the crushed ice. Garnish with a whole strawberry, serve with a straw.

### Punches, Flips, and Dessert/Temperance Cocktails

**Café con Leche Flip** — 1 oz (30 ml) Cruzan blackstrap rum, 1 oz (30 ml) coffee liqueur (Café Lolita), ¾ oz (22 ml) Simple Syrup, ¾ oz (22 ml) heavy cream, 1 egg yolk (medium egg) — shaken first without ice to emulsify, then shaken vigorously with ice, strained into a chilled sour glass. Garnish with freshly grated nutmeg.

**Dominicana** — 1½ oz (45 ml) coffee liqueur (Café Lolita), 1½ oz (45 ml) aged Dominican rum, stirred with ice, strained into a chilled coupe, floated with a thin layer of fresh whipped cream. (One of the very first drinks Sasha created at Milk & Honey.)

**Croque M. Petraske / Croque Mme Moger** — the house cocktail-hour aperitif ritual: "Monsieur" = 1½ oz (45 ml) Campari + 4 oz (120 ml) club soda over 2 medium ice cubes in a frozen highball glass; "Madame" = the same, plus 1 oz (30 ml) gin (preferably Plymouth), garnished with an orange twist if available.

**Faker Face** — ¾ oz (22 ml) fresh lime juice, ¾ oz (22 ml) grenadine syrup, 1 oz (30 ml) fresh orange juice, shaken with ice, strained into a coupe and topped with club soda. Garnish with a large blackberry balanced on two toothpicks across the rim.

**House Ginger Beer** (temperance/nonalcoholic) — 1 oz (30 ml) fresh lemon juice, 1 oz (30 ml) fresh pineapple juice, 1 oz (30 ml) Ginger Syrup, 1 bar spoon maple syrup, a dash of Angostura bitters, shaken vigorously, combined with 3 oz (90 ml) club soda and poured into a Collins glass filled with ice cubes. (Sasha's formula for temperance drinks generally: double the non-alcoholic ingredients of an existing cocktail template — Gimlet, Collins, Buck — to build a satisfying alcohol-free base.)

**Wedding Punch** (large format, makes ~2½ gallons/10.75 L) — 1 (750 ml) bottle elderflower liqueur (St-Germain), 4 (750 ml) bottles dry vermouth (Martini Bianco), 8–10 (750 ml) bottles prosecco, 8 oz (230 g) assorted berries, 1 orange sliced into wheels, 2 lemons sliced into wheels, 2 limes sliced into wheels — combined in a punch bowl large enough for an 8-inch (20 cm) ice block, garnished with the fruit. (Served at the Petraskes' own wedding.)

## GENERAL NOTES & CONVENTIONS (pp. viii, 1–9)

- **Salt**: recipes use fine salt unless noted. Weight per Tbsp varies by brand/type: Diamond Crystal kosher 9.6 g/Tbsp (2.9 g/tsp); La Baleine sea salt coarse 18.74 g/Tbsp; La Baleine fine 16.63 g/Tbsp; Morton kosher coarse 17.63 g/Tbsp; Morton sea salt coarse 17.63 g/Tbsp; Morton Mediterranean sea salt fine 16.33 g/Tbsp; Morton table salt (iodized) 18.23 g/Tbsp (5.57 g/tsp); Morton table salt (plain) 19.5 g/Tbsp (5.67 g/tsp). **Weighing ingredients is strongly recommended over volume.**
- Salt/yeast amounts sometimes given to tenths of a gram — use an analytical scale (accurate to 1/10,000 g) for very small quantities; dilute into the water portion for even dispersal.
- Butter used in recipes: unsalted, 82–83% fat, added at room temperature.
- Malted flours contain diastatic malt powder (DMP), boosting amylase/sugar for yeast. DMP is added even to malted-flour doughs with long ferments, to ensure yeast never runs out of food.
- "Sugar" in ingredient lists always means granulated sugar.
- Baker's percentage symbol used throughout (flour = 100%).

### How To Scale a Recipe (p.1)
1. Note flour = 100% in the baker's-percentage column.
2. Recipe Conversion Factor (RCF) = desired yield ÷ recipe yield.
3. New flour weight = original flour weight ÷ RCF.
4. New ingredient weight = ingredient's baker's % × new flour weight.
5. For items with no baker's %, multiply the count/volume by the RCF.
(Worked example: 1.4 kg dough → 1 kg dough, RCF = 0.71; 630 g flour → 447 g; water at 76.19% → 340 g; salt at 2.94% → 13.1 g.)

### Commercial Yeast (p.1)
- **Instant yeast**: can go straight into flour, but blooming in room-temp water (19–21°C/66–70°F) improves dispersal. Skipping bloom → proofing time +20–30 min; in low-hydration doughs (≤55%) some granules may remain visibly undissolved.
- **Active dry yeast**: must bloom in 43°C/110°F water (warm liquid needed to slough dead cell layer). Dough will ferment faster since water starts warm — watch proofing time.
- **Fresh yeast**: dissolve in room-temp water portion by hand/whisk.

### Poolish (p.2)
Table — yeast amount vs. time before mixing into dough (storage 21–24°C/70–75°F):
| Start poolish before mixing | % instant yeast |
|---|---|
| 3 h | 0.4–0.5% |
| 8 h | 0.23–0.33% |
| 12–16 h | 0.1–0.2% |
| 17–18 h | 0.01–0.09% |

Ripens any time between 3–18 h; older poolish = better structure/more fermentation flavor. Ripe poolish shows CO2 bubbles, elevated height, bubbles rupture when moved, sweetish/fresh-milk aroma. Overripe poolish can still be used +0.5% commercial yeast, frozen, or converted to a levain.

### Levain (pp. 3–5)
Comparison of levain types:
| Type | Inoculation time | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Liquid levain | 4–5 d | basic ingredients; biological expression of environment; economical | needs maintenance/temp control; excess levain produced |
| Commercial yeast/poolish-started levain | 3–4 d | shorter inoculation than liquid levain | needs maintenance; slower to develop levain flavor |
| Florapan | 18 h @ 28°C/82°F or 24 h @ 21°C/70°F | fresh daily; pronounced sour <1 day | less complex flavor |

**Starting a liquid levain** (yield 200 g): 100 g bread flour (11.5–12% protein) + 100 g water (100%/100% baker's %). Mix, cover airtight, hold at ~21°C/70°F. Wait 48 h for first CO2 activity. Discard 75% of culture, refeed equal parts flour/water (up to 120% water) at 24°C/75°F; repeat daily at consistent time. After day 4, discarded levain can be used or frozen (Second-Chance Levain).

**Best Bets for Levains** table (storage temp / ripeness windows):
- Liquid levain @ 13°C/55°F (wine fridge): 10–12h young/mellow sour, 14–16h mature, 18–20h ripe/pronounced sour, 25–27h no longer usable.
- Liquid levain @ 21–24°C/70–75°F: 8–10h young, 12–14h mature, 16–18h ripe, 23–25h spent.
- Frozen levain @ -10°C/14°F: not fed, very sour when thawed; needs 0.4–0.5% added instant yeast; freeze up to 2 months.
- Feed schedule: daily (once/day cool months, twice/day hot months or hot environments). Composition ratio 100% flour : 100% water : 25% liquid levain (of previous batch).
- Short-term storage (2–3 d): feed then refrigerate, or feed+keep at 10°C/50°F, or feed with reduced hydration to 60% + 2% salt at room temp.
- Beyond 3 d: feed, then freeze (≤2 mo) or dehydrate.

**Dehydrating a levain**: spread thin on nonstick mat, dehydrator at 30°C/86°F (lowest setting), dry ≥24 h, grind dried levain in coffee/spice grinder, store airtight in fridge up to 1 year. To revive: 100% water rehydrate 48 h at room temp, then feed 100% flour + 100% water; active again within 24 h. Best dehydrated at peak ripeness, 1.5–18 h after feeding.

**Second-Chance (inactive/frozen) levain**: provides flavor + hydrated flour only, NOT leavening — must add 0.4–0.5% instant yeast. Thaw 2–3 h in 29°C/85°F water bath, or thaw cubes 18+ h at room temp. Never let inactive levain exceed 60°C/140°F (starch gelatinizes, becomes unusable).

## CHAPTER 5: PIZZA OVENS — Best Oven by Style (pp. 10–11)

| Pizza style (bake temp range) | Best oven | Compromise | Not recommended |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Neapolitan** (455–480°C / 850–900°F) | Gas-fired pizza oven | Wood-fired, pizza deck, impinger oven | Bread deck, combi, convection, home oven |
| **Artisan (high-heat)** (250–315°C / 480–600°F) | Pizza deck, bread deck, impinger oven | Wood-fired, gas-fired | n/a |
| **Thin-Crust** (250–285°C / 480–550°F) | Pizza deck, bread deck, gas-fired, impinger | Combi, convection, wood-fired, home oven | n/a |
| **Brazilian Thin-Crust** (250–285°C / 480–550°F) | same as Thin-Crust | same | n/a |
| **New York** (290–315°C / 550–600°F) | Pizza deck, bread deck, gas-fired, impinger | Combi, convection, wood-fired, home oven | n/a |
| **Artisan (medium-heat)** (250–315°C / 480–600°F) | Pizza deck, bread deck, impinger | Combi, convection, home oven | n/a |
| **Focaccia** (205–260°C / 400–500°F) | Pizza deck, bread deck, impinger, home oven | Combi, convection | Wood-fired, gas-fired |
| **New York Square** (205–260°C / 400–500°F) | Pizza deck, bread deck, impinger | Combi, convection, home oven | Wood-fired, gas-fired |
| **Al Taglio** (250–315°C / 480–600°F) | Pizza deck, bread deck, impinger | Home oven | Combi, convection, wood-fired, gas-fired |
| **Detroit-Style** (250–275°C / 480–525°F) | Pizza deck, bread deck, impinger | Combi, convection, home oven | Wood-fired, gas-fired |
| **Deep-Dish** (250–290°C / 480–550°F) | Pizza deck, bread deck, impinger | Combi, convection, home oven | Wood-fired, gas-fired |

Key notes: Neapolitan needs the highest heat (up to 900°F) and is only well-served by gas-fired ovens where temperature is controllable; wood-fired ovens have inconsistent fires and require skill. Pan-baked styles (Detroit, deep-dish, al taglio) are hard to rotate/move in wood/gas pizza ovens — better in deck ovens. For combi/convection/home ovens, use a baking steel for a crispy bottom, and a heat gun on the rim for char if needed (Neapolitan).

### How To Preheat a Home Oven (p.11)
1. Calibrate oven — keep an oven thermometer inside at all times.
2. Place baking steel/stone in oven BEFORE turning on to preheat.
3. Position rack in top third of oven.
4. Turn oven on; turn OFF convection fan (need static heat).
5. Preheat duration depends on oven type.

## CHAPTER 6: MAKING PIZZA DOUGH — Method & Technique (pp. 12–24)

### Triangle Test (p.12)
Statistical tasting method: give each of ≥10 tasters 3 samples (2 identical + 1 different), randomize order across tasters (AAB, ABA, BAA...), have them ID the odd one out. Max 2 sets (6 samples) per session to avoid taster fatigue.

### Dough Capacity by Mixer (p.12)
| Mixer size | Dough amount |
|---|---|
| 3.8–4.7 L / 4–5 qt stand mixer | 1–1.5 kg / 2.2–3.3 lb |
| 5.7–7.6 L / 6–8 qt stand mixer | 1.5–2 kg / 3.3–4.4 lb (1.5 kg max for stiff doughs like Brazilian thin-crust) |

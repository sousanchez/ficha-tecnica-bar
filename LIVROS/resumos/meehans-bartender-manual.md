# Meehan's Bartender Manual

**Author:** Jim Meehan; photography by Doron Gild; illustrations by Gianmarco Magnani; foreword by Peter Meehan
**Publisher:** Ten Speed Press, first edition, California, 2017
**ISBN:** 9781607748625

## Structural Overview

Jim Meehan (co-founder of PDT in NYC, formerly of the Pegu Club and Gramercy Tavern) wrote this as a comprehensive operations-and-recipe manual explicitly modeled on century-old texts like Charles Mahoney's *Hoffman House Bartender's Guide* and Harry Johnson's *Bartenders' Manual* — a genre he says hadn't been attempted in over a hundred years. It differs from Death & Co-style recipe books by devoting roughly half its length to bar operations (history, design, service, hospitality, career) rather than just ingredients/technique/recipes. Structure (8 chapters + front/back matter):

1. **History** — punch → ice/julep era → birth of "mixology" (1856) and Jerry Thomas → Prohibition/bartender diaspora to Europe & Cuba → Repeal and decline → post-WWII "Age of Efficiency" (vodka, tiki) → 1960s–70s "Dark Ages" → 1987 craft cocktail revival (Dale DeGroff/Rainbow Room) → Sasha Petraske/Milk & Honey → the author's own entry into the trade (2002, PDT era).
2. **Bar Design** — location/concept/branding/interior design/décor/environment (lighting, music, HVAC)/the physical bar (dimensions, ice wells, workstation ergonomics). Includes annotated floor plans of five real bars: NoMad Bar and Library (NYC), Dead Rabbit Taproom (NYC), Lidkoeb (Copenhagen), Bryant's Cocktail Lounge (Milwaukee), Drink (Boston).
3. **Tools & Techniques** — built around one deliberately over-complicated eleven-drink table order (Absinthe Drip, Mint Julep, Pisco Sour, Michelada, Margarita, Green Thumb, Negroni, Bloody Mary, Piña Colada, Irish Coffee, Jimmie Roosevelt), walking through mise en place, delegating between "point" and "service" bartenders, tools (shakers, mixing glasses, barspoons, strainers, jiggers), ice, glassware, shaking/stirring/muddling/throwing-rolling physics (leaning heavily on Dave Arnold's *Liquid Intelligence*), egg whites, dilution, straining, garnishing, and service choreography.
4. **Distillery Tour** — a cross-category walk through production (growing/harvesting → milling/mashing → fermentation → distillation → barrel maturation → blending → filtration → bottling → tasting), illustrated with real distillery visits (Bruichladdich, Yamazaki, Maker's Mark, Woodford Reserve, Cameronbridge/Tanqueray, St. George Spirits, Bacardi's Tultitlán bottling hall, Chichicapa mezcal palenque, Leopold Brothers).
5. **Spirits & Cocktails** (the book's core, ~250 pages, subdivided into the categories below) — "one hundred recipes... my cocktail canon," each with **Origin / Logic / Hacks** essay-format entries plus a modern spec (exact ounces, glass, garnish), preceded by a substantial historical/production primer for each spirit family.
6. **Cocktail Menu** — menu design as business plan: space/cost constraints, core vs. seasonal vs. limited-run recipes, the "Mr. Potato Head" and "Jenga" theories of recipe development, cocktail naming, going global, the printed menu (physical design, theft, pricing psychology), transparency about spirits-company sponsorship.
7. **Service** — full front-of-house choreography: greeting, seating, the "in and the out" of menu dialogue, pour/clear/garnish mechanics (2 o'clock garnish, 7 o'clock straw, open-handed service), noise discipline, check presentation, privacy/confidentiality, conflict de-escalation, cutting off intoxicated guests.
8. **Hospitality** — distinguished from "service" as an instinct vs. a trained skill; management philosophy (excellence vs. perfection, per Danny Meyer), building/retaining teams (sprinters vs. marathoners), company culture, bartender self-care and burnout, career trajectory beyond the bar.
9. **Epilogue: Beyond Bars** — the modern bartending career path (media, brand ambassadorship, competitions, entrepreneurship), homework/continuing education, networking, conventions (Tales of the Cocktail, Bar Convent Berlin), the "startender" critique.
10. **Bibliography** (~90 titles) and **Subrecipes** appendix (10 house syrups/batters: simple, Demerara, honey, vanilla, and agave syrups; ginger wort; lime cordial; whipped cream; horchata; Tom and Jerry batter).

Throughout, roughly 50 sidebar interviews (a paragraph of pull-quote each) with named industry figures — bartenders (Dale DeGroff, Audrey Saunders, Sasha Petraske's peers, Dave Arnold, Jeff Bell, Julie Reiner, Jack McGarry), historians (David Wondrich, Dave Broom), master distillers/blenders (John Glaser, Hans Reisetbauer, Beppe Musso, Dan Farber), and others (photographer Daniel Krieger, sommelier Jordan Salcito) — function as a kind of oral-history Rolodex of the 2000s–2010s cocktail renaissance.

## The Spirits & Cocktails Chapter — Category by Category

Each spirit section opens with an origin/production essay, then delivers cocktails in the essay format described above. Selected specifics:

**Aperitifs** — vermouth (Carpano's 1786 Turin trademark; French "blanc" vs. dry vs. Italian rosso; author uses Dolin blanc for most pre-Prohibition "French vermouth" calls, arguing the era's French vermouth was the sweeter Chambéry style, not dry Noilly Prat); quinquinas (Dubonnet, Lillet, Byrrh — created for the French Foreign Legion to mask quinine); Campari/Aperol and the aperitivo bitters family; absinthe blanche/verte production (bain-marie pot still, 65–72% ABV); champagne/prosecco sourcing; sherry/madeira/port. Recipes: Absinthe Drip, Champagne Cocktail, Kir, Venetian Spritz, Torino Torino, Sherry Cobbler, Pimm's Cup, Bamboo, Nurse (original), Michelada.

**Vodka** — Polish/Russian origin dispute (word first appears 1405), U.S./EU legal definitions of "neutral," Western vs. Eastern-style production, the flavored-vodka boom (Stoli Limonaya 1986, Absolut Citron 1988). Recipes: Vesper (Bond's 3:1 gin-to-vodka ratio, Lillet blanc), Moscow Mule (Jack Morgan/John Martin/copper-mug marketing origin story), Parkside Fizz (original), Bloody Mary (Fernand Petiot/King Cole Bar 1934), Peaches and Cream (original), Gold Coast (original, Swedish punsch), Tatanka, Czechmate (original), Cocktail Culture (original, yogurt lassi), Cosmopolitan (Toby Cecchini, Odeon 1988).

**Gin and Genever** — full genever/gin etymological and legal history (Dutch genever → English gin craze → 1751 Gin Act → 19th-c. unsweetened London dry → Prohibition bathtub gin → 1987 Bombay Sapphire/vodka-style marketing → 2004 Simon Ford/Plymouth-led classic-gin revival → "new Western" gins from 2006). Recipes: Martini, Gin and Tonic, Gimlet, French 75, Aviation, 20th Century, Last Word (Murray Stenson/Zig Zag Café 2002 revival story), Ramos Gin Fizz, Martinez, Corpse Reviver #2, Old Friend (original), White Lady, Negroni, Hans Solo (original, barrel-aged gin), Hanky-Panky (Ada Coleman/Savoy).

**Rum and Cachaça** — sugar/slave-trade history of the Triangle Trade explicitly discussed; Jamaican "hogo" dunder fermentation; navy-strength/grog (Admiral "Old Grog" Vernon, 1740); rhum agricole AOC (Martinique, 1996); Cuban column-still ron (Bacardí, 1862); cachaça categories (classica/ouro/premium). Recipes: Green Tea Punch (original, per David Wondrich's *Punch*), Mojito, Hemingway Daiquiri, Green Thumb (original), Caipirinha, Piña Colada (Caribe Hilton, 1954), Ti' Punch, El Presidente, Daiquiri, Five Island Flamingo (original), Mai-Tai, Airmail, East India Negroni (original), Plátanos en Mole Old-Fashioned (original).

**Tequila and Mezcal** — Denominación de Origen states, mixto vs. 100% agave, jimador harvesting, horno/tahona vs. autoclave/diffuser processing, Chinaco/El Tesoro/Patrón import history (1980s), Ron Cooper/Del Maguey coining "single-village mezcal," Phil Ward's Oaxaca Old-Fashioned popularizing mezcal (2007, Death & Co). Recipes: Paloma, Tommy's Margarita (Julio Bermejo, ~1987–88), Margarita, Rosita, 21st Century (original, Pegu Club), Witch's Kiss (original), El Diablo, Mezcal Mule (original), San Miguel (original), Mechuga (original).

**Whisk(e)y** — broadest historical sweep: Whiskey Rebellion, Dr. James Crow's sour mash, Lincoln County Process, Coffey still (1830), Scotch blending (Andrew Usher, 1853), Bottled-in-Bond Act (1909), Japanese whisky origins (Yamazaki, 1923; Taketsuru/Nikka). Category breakdowns: Irish, Scotch (blended vs. single-malt), American (bourbon/rye/Tennessee), Canadian, Japanese. Recipes: Highball, Rosy Cheeks (original), Stone Fence, Brown Derby, Mint Julep, Blood and Sand, Hot Whiskey, Irish Coffee, Bobby Burns, Brooklyn, Manhattan, Boulevardier, Sazerac, Old-Fashioned Whiskey Cocktail, Toronto, Brown Bomber (original), Purple Rain (original), Cameron's Kick, For Peat's Sake (original, w/ Jeff Bell), George Washington (original), DuBoudreau Cocktail (original).

**Brandy and Eau de Vie** — cognac production and classification (VS/VSOP/XO, brouillis/bonne chauffe, paradis cellars), pisco (Peru: puro/acholado/mosto verde), calvados AOC, American applejack (Laird's), eau de vie (Reisetbauer's process detailed at length). Recipes: Pisco Sour, Pisco Punch, Sidecar, Jack Rose, Champs-Élysées, Newark (original), Rose, Jimmie Roosevelt, French Maid (original), Japanese Cocktail (Jerry Thomas, 1860).

**Liqueurs** — etymology of "alcohol" and "liqueur," monastic origins, Bénédictine's 27-botanical two-year production process detailed, EU sugar/ABV minimums by category. Recipes (mostly rich/nightcap-oriented): Coffee Cocktail (Jerry Thomas — cognac, port, whole egg), Rusty Nail, Stinger, Grasshopper, Tom and Jerry, After Nine (original), Café Arroz (original, horchata + tequila), Milk Punch, Black Flip (original).

Every recipe entry follows the same three-part rhetorical structure: **Origin** (sourced to specific historic bar guides — Jerry Thomas 1862/1887, Harry Craddock's *Savoy Cocktail Book* 1930, Harry McElhone's *ABC of Mixing Cocktails* 1922, David Wondrich's *Imbibe!* as the recurring authority), **Logic** (why the proportions/ingredients work, often citing Dave Arnold's *Liquid Intelligence* science), and **Hacks** (substitution guidance and named brand recommendations).

## Cross-Cutting Technique Notes (Chapter 3)

- Shaking: per Dave Arnold, shake time (8–12 seconds) matters more than technique for temperature; aeration depends on ice size and force. Boston shaker sealed at an angle (5–7 o'clock), shaken over one shoulder with the larger tin facing the guest so leaks spray backward.
- Stirring: middle/index/thumb grip near the spoon's top, back of the bowl against the glass, ice moved gently clockwise; dilution/chill depend on ice size and stir speed/duration.
- Dry-shake-then-shake-with-ice sequence for egg white drinks to emulsify before dilution.
- "Throwing" (aerating by pouring between two tins raised overhead) traced through India → Morocco → Basque cider culture → Jerez venencia sherry service → Boadas (Barcelona, 1938) → modern Bloody Mary prep.
- Ice hierarchy: Kold-Draft/Hoshizaki 1.25" cubes for shaking/stirring, Scotsman pebble ice for swizzles/juleps/blender drinks, Clinebell block ice for clear rocks/spheres.
- Garnish placement standardized at 2 o'clock (right-handed guest) with straw at 7 or 5 o'clock; default to right-handed unless cues (watch position, water glass side) suggest otherwise.
- The "Mr. Potato Head" theory (credited to Phil Ward) and the author's complementary "Jenga" analogy: swapping a same-class ingredient (base spirit) is safe; swapping an ingredient that carries two functions (e.g., Cointreau's sweetness *and* proof) requires rebalancing the whole structure or the drink "topples."

## Operations Content (Chapters 2, 6, 7, 8) — Key Specifics

- Bar top: 38–42" high, 32–38" wide including bumper/drip tray; stool seats 24–32" high with a footrest rung 18" below seat.
- Backbar set 40–50" from the inner counter edge — enough for a barback to slip behind a working bartender without forcing them to turn around.
- Menu economics: fixed-price cocktail menus (PDT model) stabilize a variable cost (liquor) against fixed costs (rent/payroll); PDT's $7 hot dogs subsidize $16 cocktails to lower average check perception.
- Staffing philosophy: "sprinters" (opening-team risk-takers) vs. "marathoners" (steady second-wave hires); one year to train + one year to "pay it back" as the informal tenure expectation.
- Career arc essay (Epilogue) traces the post-2000s professionalization of bartending into media, brand ambassadorship (Simon Ford, Charlotte Voisey held up as models), competitions (Diageo World Class, Bacardi Legacy, Speed Rack), and entrepreneurship (Small Hand Foods, The 86 Co., Jack Rudy, Aviation Gin) — while cautioning against "startenders" who skip the craft apprenticeship.

## Relation to Other Titles in This Collection

Meehan's overlaps in period and cast of characters with *Death & Co* (both ~2014–2017, both citing Phil Ward's Oaxaca Old-Fashioned, David Wondrich, and Dave Arnold as touchstones) but is structured as an operations manual with essayistic history first and recipes second, whereas *Death & Co* is organized recipe-forward around ingredients/tools/technique/creation-methodology. Meehan's is also unusual among this shelf's bar books for spending nearly half its page count on things outside the glass — real estate, floor plans, staffing, menu economics, and career sociology of bartending as a profession.

# Death & Co: Welcome Home — A Cocktail Recipe Book

**Authors:** Alex Day, Nick Fauchald & David Kaplan (Death & Co)
**Publisher:** Ten Speed Press / Penguin Random House, 2021 — ISBN 9781984858412

This is Death & Co's third book, and second full cocktail-recipe volume after the original *Death & Co: Modern Classic Cocktails* (2014) and the technique-focused *Cocktail Codex* (2018). It documents the company's evolution from a single NYC bar (opened New Year's Eve 2006) into a multi-city hospitality company (Death & Co New York, Denver, and Los Angeles, run alongside consulting firm Proprietors LLC), written substantially during the COVID-19 shutdown of 2020. Framed as a new-hire "employee handbook," the book is explicitly structured to walk a reader through the same onboarding arc as a rookie Death & Co bartender: philosophy and setup → ingredient literacy → menu-creation process → home application → the recipes themselves.

## Structural Overview

The book has five chapters plus appendices, meant to be read in order (each chapter builds on the last), though the authors say you can skip straight to the recipes:

1. **Preparation** — mindset, physical bar setup, tools, glassware, ice, garnishes, core technique (jiggering, shaking, stirring, straining), and an intro to cocktail-creation philosophy.
2. **Selection** — how to taste and evaluate spirits and modifiers; a spirit-by-spirit reference (raw materials, geography, categories, aging, "in cocktails" use) covering vodka, gin, tequila, mezcal, rum, American whiskey, Scotch/Irish whiskey, unaged brandy, aged brandy; then modifiers (liqueurs, amari, fortified wines) and bitters.
3. **Development** — how Death & Co actually designs a bar menu, from intellectual concept through physical testing to finalization/launch.
4. **Cocktails at Home** — adapting bar practice to the home: a curated 12-bottle/50-cocktail starter bar, "dusty bottle" ideas, batching strategies for groups, the "Freezer Bar," carbonation, and a full set of home-bartender substitution rules.
5. **Specs** — the recipe collection itself (roughly 500 recipes), organized into six named categories.

Appendices cover **Bar Prep Recipes & Techniques** (syrups/cordials, infusions, house bitters/mixes/juices/shrubs, solutions/tinctures, garnishes) followed by Resources, a recommended-reading "Bookshelf," Acknowledgments, and an Index. Scattered through the main text are "The Regulars" sidebars — interviews with bar regulars, each paired with one of their favorite recipes.

## House Philosophy & Techniques

### Core philosophy: "intention" and "Always Be Knolling" (ABK)
The book's guiding word is **intention** — having a clear vision of the end result before beginning any task, from building one drink to designing a whole menu. This is paired with **"Always Be Knolling" (ABK)**, borrowed from artist Tom Sachs's *Ten Bullets*: keep your station rigorously organized (scan for unused tools, put them away, group like objects, align everything at parallel/90°) as both a practical and mental discipline. The refrain "reset to zero" describes returning your station to its starting configuration after every drink.

### Technique progression: comprehension → fluency → speed
New skills are taught in three stages: first slow, merciless accuracy (comprehension); then repeated practice broken into isolated component motions, "isolate, then combine" (fluency); only then speed. Working fast is not the goal — muscle memory and mind-body coordination are.

### Core bar techniques (Ch. 1)
- **Jiggering:** two-handed technique, pour to a flat/level rim (not a convex meniscus — abandoned as inconsistent), ambidextrous practice.
- **Shaking:** standard is the 18-oz/28-oz tin set, 5–7 Kold-Draft-size (~1") cubes, circular ice motion (not back-and-forth piston), 10–15 seconds total (build to top speed ~5 sec, hold, decelerate). Variants: **short shake** (~5 sec, for drinks going over ice or topped with something bubbly), **whip shake** (with a few crushed-ice pebbles, until ice fully melts, for crushed-ice drinks), **dry shake** (no ice, for egg-white drinks, then shaken again with ice).
- **Stirring:** ice built as an "upside-down pyramid" in a pint glass, ~15 seconds, taste via straw or back-of-hand.
- **Straining:** julep strainer or Hawthorne for stirred drinks; Hawthorne (with "gate control") mandatory for shaken drinks; **double straining** (Hawthorne + fine mesh cone strainer) for most shaken up-drinks.
- **Rule of thumb — shaken vs. stirred:** anything with citrus, egg, or cream is shaken; anything mostly spirituous (vermouth, bitters, small amounts of sugar) is stirred.

### Ice
Categorized by function: large blocks (2½×2×2") for old-fashioned-style stirred/shaken service; Kold-Draft 1¼" cubes for shaking and collins-style service; crushed/pellet ice (Scotsman machine) for juleps, tropical drinks, swizzles, coolers. Home substitute for crushed ice: ice cubes smashed in a canvas bag with a mallet/rolling pin.

### Tasting methodology (Ch. 2)
"Smell first, taste second"; three sips with water in between (clear, introduce, contemplate); reflect and discuss using personal/memory-based language rather than jargon. A full sequence of guided "palate lessons" builds understanding via **Daiquiri variants** that isolate one variable at a time: Sweet & Sour (tart/balanced/sweet ratios), Strong vs. Weak (alcohol %), Salt (2 drops vs. 1 tsp salt solution), Bitter (incremental Campari), Temperature (frozen/room-temp/chilled), Dilution (under-/properly-/over-diluted). Spirit sections follow the same experimental pattern (e.g., three vodkas in a stripped-down vodka soda then vodka sour; three gins in a Tom Collins then a Negroni).

### Menu development process (Ch. 3)
Death & Co's menus are split into three phases: **Intellectual Development** (wireframe spreadsheet: section, cocktail, Style, Flavor Profile, Garnish, Glassware, Bartender, Ice — filled in collaboratively before any liquid is poured), **Physical Development** (recipe testing with a "five tries and you're out" rule; team tastings ~7 weeks and ~1–2 weeks before launch), and **Finalization** (naming, writing menu copy, costing, batching/station-mapping, prep lists, and a literal hour-by-hour "Menu Launch" day schedule from 3am cleanup to 3am reopening-day close).

Named creative strategies for inventing drinks: **The Batchelor** (repurposing a service batch as its own drink), **New Toy!** (built around a novel ingredient), **Liquid Lunch** (inspired by a dish), **Super Mr. Potato Head** (swapping components in a past Death & Co drink, not just a classic), **Fancy Flex** (a "luxury" section built around one expensive ingredient), **I'll Just Have a Beer** (beer-flavor-profile drinks), **Prep School Standout** (starting from a syrup/infusion idea), **Uncharted Territory** ("what if…" experiments). Also "Flavor Syllogisms" (A loves B, B loves C, does C love A?) as a brainstorming device, illustrated with a worked table of examples.

**No-ABV strategies:** purées as base (body/texture), verjus as base+balancer, nonalcoholic "spirit" replacements (Seedlip, Giffard Aperitif Syrup), no-ABV milk punch. **Low-ABV strategies:** split base (spirit + lower-proof modifier), "flip the script" (invert spirit:vermouth ratio), riffing on classics like the Bamboo.

### The six-category menu structure
Since roughly 2018, all Death & Co menus (and this book's recipe chapter) use six categories, each anchored to one of the six "root cocktails" from *Cocktail Codex*:

| Category | Root cocktail | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Fresh & Lively | Highball | Highball/spritz variations |
| Light & Playful | Daiquiri | Simple sours |
| Bright & Confident | Sidecar | Complex sours (liqueur-driven) |
| Boozy & Honest | Old-Fashioned | Old-Fashioned and variations |
| Elegant & Timeless | Martini | Martini, Manhattan, Negroni riffs |
| Rich & Comforting | Flip | Decadent/rich (flips, tiki, piña colada-style) |

Within each section, drinks run low-to-high proof, and each section is required to contain at least one no-ABV and one low-ABV option — a deliberate hospitality/wayfinding device.

### Home adaptation (Ch. 4)
**12 bottles → 50 cocktails** starter bar: Beefeater gin, Flor de Caña 4-yr white rum, El Tesoro Blanco tequila, Famous Grouse blended scotch, Elijah Craig 12-yr bourbon, Busnel VSOP calvados, Paul Beau VS cognac, Campari, Amaro Averna, Cointreau, Cocchi Vermouth di Torino, Dolin dry vermouth — plus bitters, syrups, seltzer/sparkling wine, citrus, egg/dairy/spices. "Always start with a Daiquiri."

**Dusty Bottles** framework for using underused ingredients, with five roles to try a bottle in: Gateway Cocktail, Same Same But Different, Star of the Show, Supporting Character, Unexpected Use (plus ★ for a true classic) — demonstrated across absinthe, Aperol, aquavit, blackstrap rum, cachaça, Chartreuse, crème de cacao, Islay scotch, Kalani coconut liqueur, maraschino, menthe-pastille, pear eau-de-vie, pisco, Suze.

**Batching spectrum for entertaining**, least to most complex:
- *Minimally batched* ("choose your own"): DIY stations (Old-Fashioned Your Way, High Five Your Way, Bellini/Mimosa Your Way, Bloody Mary Your Way) where guests pick a spirit and combine with a pre-made base.
- *Partially batched*: combine stable alcoholic ingredients ahead; add fragile ingredients (egg, aquafaba, effervescence) to order. Rule of thumb: shake up to 2 cocktails per shaker tin (3 if champagne-topped, since bubbly adds most of the volume).
- *Fully batched*: all ingredients combined ahead (max 6 hrs, chilled) — used for punches. Punch built as a scaled-up Tom Collins-style citrus+spirit+bubbles construction, served over one large block, not chips.
- **Carbonated cocktails**: three variables — temperature (chill everything cold before charging), clarity (fresh citrus doesn't carbonate cleanly; use acid solutions — citric/lactic/malic/phosphoric — or clarified juice instead), dilution (aim for 50–60% water/effervescent content in the final drink).
- **The Freezer Bar**: batch a spirituous drink (≥30% ABV total so it won't freeze at ~0°F/-1.75°F freezing point) plus a calculated water addition (10–15% for vermouth-based Manhattan/Martini-style; ~20% for Old-Fashioned/Negroni-style since they're diluted further on the rocks anyway), then store and pour to order. Bitters intensify in large batches — halve the recipe amount in batches over 5 drinks, then adjust to taste. Freezer batches can themselves become base spirits for spritzes, highballs, sours/fizzes/collinses, or be flavor-infused (iSi pressure-infusion or 24-hr steep) — e.g., Strawberry Negroni, Meyer Lemon Martini, Chocolate Manhattan, Celery Martini.

**Home Bartender Adaptability Rules**: measurement substitutes (1 tbsp = ½ oz), fixing a botched pour by rebalancing the other ingredients proportionally rather than starting over; ice-quantity fixes for under-chilled stirred/shaken drinks; freezing all stemware; skipping the mixing glass (build directly in an old-fashioned glass over one large cube); spirit-type and spirit-brand substitution rules (stay within style: gin for gin not genever, blanco rum for blanco not aged, etc.); glassware substitution priorities (Nick & Nora as the single most versatile glass if you can only own one stemmed glass).

## Recipe Organization Scheme

Chapter 5 ("Specs") presents roughly 500 recipes as a flat alphabetical list *within* each of the six category files (no further subdivision by spirit). Each recipe entry includes:
- Title, creator name and year
- Optional short first-person "why I made this" quote from the bartender
- Ingredient list (in order added — but note the "Working in Zones" sidebar explains bartenders actually build back-to-front from cheapest/smallest to priciest during service)
- Garnish line
- Method paragraph (shake/stir/build, glass, ice, garnish execution)

**Icon tags** mark special recipe attributes, used throughout chapter 5:
- **LOW-ABV**
- **NO-ABV**
- **FREEZER BAR** (safe to batch-and-freeze)
- **PROJECT COCKTAIL** (multi-step/equipment-heavy)
- **LOW-PREP COCKTAIL** (minimal advance prep)

A short "bitters note" applies globally: recipe bitters amounts assume standard retail dasher bottles; double the dash count if using fancier/wider dasher bottles.

## Bar Prep: Techniques & Method Sections (Appendix)

**Syrups & Cordials** — dozens of recipes, almost all by weight in grams. Two base methods dominate:
- *Cold-blend syrups* (e.g., Cane Sugar Syrup: 300g sugar : 150g water, blended until dissolved) — for stable dry sugars.
- *Sous vide (immersion-circulator) syrups* (e.g., Cinnamon Syrup, Demerara Syrup, Grapefruit Cordial, Alpenglow Cordial): ingredients vacuum/press-sealed in a bag, cooked 1–2 hrs at 130–145°F, ice-bathed, then fine-strained (with a paper filter or "Superbag" as needed for full clarity).

**Sous Vide Infusions — full method:**
1. Fill basin, set immersion circulator to target temp (typically 135–145°F: lower for delicate ingredients like fruit, higher for sturdy ones like nuts/spices).
2. Weigh ingredients into a bowl, transfer to a sealable heatproof bag, press out air by water-displacement sealing.
3. Cook in the bath for the specified time (usually ~2 hrs).
4. Ice-bath to cool (this recondenses vapor and preserves ABV).
5. Strain through fine mesh, then paper filter/Superbag if needed; refrigerate.

**Pressurized (iSi) Infusions — full method:** for fragile, fast-perishing flavors (herbs) or ingredients with wide flavor ranges (cacao nibs). Ingredients loaded into a 1-qt iSi whipper below the max line, charged with two N₂O cartridges (shaking between), rested under pressure ~10 min, then rapidly vented and strained. Because extraction is so fast there's less risk of off-flavors from long maceration; delicate infusions are best used within 1 week–1 month.

**Clarified Juices** — two methods:
- *Agar clarification*: juice combined with an agar-gelled portion of water (agar dosed at 0.2% of combined weight), the gelled agar broken into curds and squeezed through cheesecloth to release clear liquid, leaving cloudy solids behind. Used within 1 day (fragile) unless preserved in a batched/sweetened/spirited cocktail.
- *Centrifuge clarification*: juice dosed with Pectinex Ultra SP-L (a pectinase) then Kieselsol/Chitosan fining agents (each ~0.2% of weight, rested 15 min between additions), spun at 4,500 rpm for 12 minutes in balanced containers, then strained.

**Centrifuge Infusions (flavor extraction from solids):** solid + spirit blended (starting ratios by weight: ~1:4 for dry ingredients like dried fruit/graham cracker, ~1:2 for moist ones like banana/strawberry, ~1:1 for very subtle ones like watermelon), strained, dosed with Pectinex, spun at 4,500 rpm/12 min, strained again.

**House Bitters, Mixes, Juices & Shrubs** — includes House Orange Bitters (equal parts Fee Brothers, Angostura, and Regans' orange bitters blended together), Donn's Mix #1 (grapefruit juice + Cinnamon Syrup, the tiki-history base used throughout the book), Hibiscus Shrub (vinegar-steeped, reduced, then blended with sugar and water), Tepache (spiced fermented-pineapple base, 4-day room-temp ferment).

**Solutions & Tinctures** — simple percentage solutions used as seasoning/acid-adjustment tools: Citric Acid Solution (20% by weight in water), Malic Acid Solution (20%), Lactic Acid Solution (10%), Salt Solution (30%), Champagne Acid Solution (tartaric + lactic blend), Habanero Tincture (vodka-based, 24-hr steep).

**Garnishes** — dehydrator techniques (135°F, several hours) for dehydrated apple slices, citrus wheels, and pineapple wedges (soaked in salted water first to prevent browning); Campari-Infused Toasted Coconut (infuse then dehydrate the reserved solids); Toasted Oat Cream (toasted oats steeped in heavy cream 24 hrs, strained).

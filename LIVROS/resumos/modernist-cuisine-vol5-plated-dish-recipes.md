## 1. What This Volume Is

Volume 5 is the plated-dish recipe collection of the five-volume *Modernist Cuisine* set. Volumes 1–4 (History and Fundamentals; Techniques and Equipment; Animals and Plants; Ingredients and Preparations) build the science and component/parametric recipes; Volume 5 is where those building blocks are assembled into full composed restaurant dishes — a French-restaurant-style pattern of one "hero" protein or vegetable plus a half-dozen to a dozen named sub-recipes (sauces, gels, foams, glazes, garnishes) that are individually built, then reheated and plated together at service.

**Recipe format** (explained in the book's "About the Recipes" front matter, which repeats material from Vol. 1 p. 1-93): every dish opens with a title, an italicized tagline listing its key components, a short headnote on culinary concept/history/inspiration, then a metadata block (**Yield**, **Special Equipment**, **Optional Equipment**, **Time Required**) and an **Order of Preparation** table listing every sub-recipe with prep time, cook time, unattended time, and finishing time. Each sub-recipe then gets its own ingredient table (gram weights + baker's-percentage-style scaling column + numbered procedure), and the dish closes with an **Assembly** section describing reheating and plating.

Ingredients are weighed in grams throughout (not volume), including water and often salt, because the hydrocolloids and gelling/emulsifying agents used (xanthan gum, agar, gellan, sodium alginate, transglutaminase/Activa, methylcellulose, etc.) are dose-sensitive — "0.1% more or less... can ruin some recipes." Temperatures are given in °C/°F; sous vide baths are specified to the degree because, per the authors, "one of the central themes of Modernist cooking is that exact temperature control is called for."

The book explicitly credits recipes as **"by,"** **"inspired by,"** or **"adapted from"** named chefs (Ferran Adrià, Heston Blumenthal, Alain Passard, Alain Senderens, Wylie Dufresne, David Kinch, Michel Bras, Fredy Girardet, Paul Liebrandt, Andoni Luis Aduriz, Yoshihiro Murata, and others) — attribution runs to the chef/team, not the restaurant, since chefs move between restaurants and lead teams.

### CHAPTER 19 — TENDER CUTS

#### 1. Beef Rib Steak
*Shiitake marmalade, creamed watercress, aged beef jus*

Most cooks overcook beef destined for jus/consommé on the theory that high heat is needed for flavor and body; this recipe instead makes a jus **sous vide**, at rare-beef temperature, to capture the essence of the meat rather than boiling it away. **Yield** 4 | **Equipment** sous vide, juicer, gas blowtorch, whipping siphon, Pacojet (centrifuge optional) | **Time** 8 h.

- **Aged Rare Beef Jus** (250 g) — aged beef chuck + bromelain enzyme (pineapple-derived, boosts juice yield), sous vide 53°C/127°F for 4 h, pressed, then **centrifuged at 27,500g for 30 min** to clarify; thickened with xanthan + guar gum, seasoned with MSG.
- **Shiitake Marmalade** (150 g) — shallot/shiitake/garlic/beef-marrow base folded into a brown-beef-jus reduction, bound with 160 Bloom gelatin and xanthan gum.
- **Sous Vide Beef Rib Steak** (800 g) — prime aged rib steak + suet, sous vide to core 53°C/127°F (rare) / 55°C/131°F (medium-rare) / 57°C/135°F (pink), bath set 1°C/2°F above target.
- **Potato Puree** (700 g) — Yukon Gold juice **centrifuged at 27,500g for 1 h**, sous vide 90°C/194°F 20 min; combined with clarified-butter-toasted instant potato flakes and Ultra-Sperse A, folded with baked/sieved potato flesh — engineered to keep concentrated raw-potato flavor despite the fat load.
- **Suet Mousseline** (300 g) — beef-stock/wine reduction set with low-acyl gellan (Kelcogel F) into a fluid gel, blended with sous vide egg yolk (62°C/144°F, 35 min), emulsified with warm beef suet, charged in a 1 L whipping siphon (2× N₂O), held warm as a foam.
- **Salt Gel** (450 g) — salt + agar cast in a mold, grated tableside so salt seasons without dissolving.
- **Toasted Garlic Chips** (optional) — sliced garlic blanched 3× in hot milk (mellows bite), deep-fried 160°C/320°F.

**Standout technique:** bromelain-assisted sous vide beef jus, centrifuged twice in the dish (jus + potato juice); gellan fluid-gel suet emulsion whipped into a warm siphon foam.

**Plating:** steak torch-seared (high-heat MAPP/oxyacetylene torch recommended — small butane torches "burn dirty"), rested, sliced 5 mm thick from the bone; garnished with garlic chips, rosemary blossoms, fresh green peppercorns, wild watercress; mousseline, creamed watercress, marmalade, potato purée and jus served alongside; salt gel grated over the steak table-side.

#### 2. Mushroom Swiss Burger
*Emmental cheese single, mushroom ketchup, shallot rings*

A survey of American burger styles (Louis' Lunch's austere patty, Daniel Boulud's foie-gras burger, White Castle, In-N-Out) precedes "a no-holds-barred Modernist cheeseburger" built on a juicy mushroom-beef patty. **Yield** 4 | **Equipment** sous vide, meat grinder (4 mm plate) | **Time** 30 h (20 h prep).

- **Hamburger Buns** (12) — sponge fermented 24–48 h refrigerated; dough uses L-cysteine to relax gluten for a "flowing" texture and White Lily soft-wheat flour; proofed 85% RH/40°C, topped with poppy/onion/sesame seed, baked 260°C/500°F 6 min.
- **Mushroom Ketchup** (750 g) — crimini + onion, malt vinegar, dark ale, mushroom broth, barley malt syrup, molasses, fish sauce, freeze-dried shiitake powder, horseradish, mace, allspice, freeze-dried coffee powder; thickened with xanthan gum.
- **Restructured Emmental Slices** (350 g) — sodium citrate + iota/kappa carrageenan hydrated in wheat ale, blended into melted Emmental/Comté, cast in a cylinder, sliced — a from-scratch "processed cheese slice."
- **Mushroom Broth** (250 g) — shallot + crimini **pressure-cooked at 1.4 bar/21 psi for 45 min**.
- **Hamburger Glaze** (100 g) — brown beef stock + tomato confit + rendered beef suet + smoked salt, reduced.
- **Sautéed Maitake Mushroom** — in rendered beef suet.
- **Smoked Lettuce** (90 g) — iceberg vacuum-chamber-infused with liquid-smoke water (pulled until the water boils, then held 20 min) — smoke flavor with no smoker.
- **Compressed Tomato** (300 g) — beefsteak tomato vacuum-sealed to compress texture/flavor.
- Also referenced: Tomato Confit, Brown Beef Stock, Short-Rib Patty, Freeze-Dried Shiitake, Ultrasonic Fries, Strawberry Milk Shake, Onion Rings (cross-referenced component recipes elsewhere in the book).

**Standout technique:** vacuum-chamber "compression smoking" of lettuce; sodium-citrate/carrageenan restructured cheese slice; ground dry ice makes the optional strawberry milkshake bubble tableside.

**Plating:** patties griddled; buns brushed with suet and toasted; onion rings dredged in tapioca starch/egg/onion-cracker breading, fried 200°C/390°F; cheese melted on in the last 90 s; bun bottoms brushed with ketchup, tops with glaze; stacked with lettuce, tomato, mushroom, patty; served with onion rings and (optionally) a bubbling dry-ice milkshake.

#### 3. Autumn Harvest Pork Roast
*Chestnut confit, prune coals, pain d'épices*

Solves the crispy-skin-vs-overcooked-meat problem by cooking skin and loin separately, then **cryosearing the sous vide loin in liquid nitrogen** immediately before a crusted deep-fry — puffed, crackling skin over evenly cooked meat. Gascon flavors (Armagnac, prunes, chestnuts, quatre épices) frame the dish; chef Grant Crilly's braised prunes are encased in a sugar glass from the braising liquid and foamed to resemble glowing embers. **Yield** 4 | **Equipment** sous vide, pressure cooker, siphon (freeze dryer/LN₂ optional) | **Time** 49 h.

- **Braised Bacon** (250 g) — home-cured bacon sous vide 60°C/140°F 12 h.
- **Charred Leek Oil** (optional, 70 g) — whole leek charred at 120°C/250°F, blended with hot grapeseed oil + Banyuls vinegar, oil decanted.
- **Chestnut Confit** (200 g) — sous vide 90°C/194°F 12 h with pork fat and salt.
- **Sweet-and-Sour Cipolline Onion** (80 g) — sous vide 90°C/194°F 1 h 50 min with vinegar and chestnut honey.
- **Salted Caramel Apple Puree** (200 g) — dark caramel deglazed with cider; apples sous vide 90°C/194°F 20 h in the syrup, then puréed with xanthan gum.
- **Banyuls Glaze** (300 g) — ground pork + trotter roasted, sweated vegetables, **pressure-cooked with Banyuls wine at 1.4 bar/21 psi for 1.5 h**, reduced to a glaze.
- **Chestnut Puffs** (430 g) — choux-style dough (gelatin, butter, bacon fat, chestnut purée) cast, baked, deep-fried until puffed; injected via siphon with Chestnut Cream (300 g, thickened iota carrageenan + konjac gum, whipped in a siphon) and dusted with pain d'épices powder.
- **Pain d'Épices Powder** (300 g) — toasted cinnamon/star anise/clove/aniseed/coriander ground with honey powder, ginger, nutmeg, mace, orange zest.
- **Spiced Ash** (80 g) — pain d'épices powder + carbon color powder + kaolin clay for an edible black "ash," bound with hazelnut oil and N-Zorbit M maltodextrin.
- **Pork Loin Roast** (900 g) — rack cured 48 h in salt + pain d'épices; skin reserved (gelatinized/dried/minced); loin coated in Crisp Coat + Methocel E4M paste, sous vide, **dipped in liquid nitrogen 30 s** (cryosear), dusted in starch, rolled in whipped methylcellulose + minced pork skin, deep-fried 185°C/365°F until puffed.

**Standout technique:** liquid-nitrogen cryosearing of a fully cooked sous vide loin right before a methylcellulose/pork-skin-crusted fry; kaolin clay + carbon powder as edible black "ash"; siphon-foamed prune "coals."

**Plating:** loin rested 3 min, sliced between bones; chestnut puffs filled and dusted; plate finished with warm Banyuls glaze, apple purée, chestnut confit, cipolline onions, prune coals, and bacon; spiced ash dusted over the whole plate.

#### 4. Rack of Lamb with Garlic
*Seaweed tapenade, garlic nougatine, caramelized crème fraîche*

Celebrates *agneau de pré-salé* (salt-marsh lamb), paired with seaweed tapenade and an oyster beignet to evoke coastal terroir; explains that smashing garlic triggers sulfur-compound-producing enzymes that cooking mellows into meatier aromas. **Yield** 4 | **Equipment** sous vide, pressure cooker, whipping siphon | **Time** 12 h.

- **Pickled Garlic** (75 g) — blanched 3 min (deactivates the sulfur-producing enzymes) then pickled 12 h+.
- **Seaweed Tapenade** (525 g) — rehydrated dulse/arame/hijiki + green olives, shallot, caper, anchovy, Dijon, lemon, garlic; thickened with xanthan gum.
- **Caramelized Crème Fraîche** (300 g) — crème fraîche + baking soda **pressure-cooked at 1.4 bar/21 psi for 2.5 h** to force Maillard browning without dry heat; blended with sieved garlic confit and Ultra-Sperse 3.
- **Oyster Beignet** (4) — tapioca/flour/baking-soda/xanthan batter aerated in a siphon (N₂O), coats Kumamoto oysters, deep-fried 200°C/392°F.
- **Garlic Nougatine** (400 g) — glucose syrup DE40 + isomalt with butter, flour, blanched garlic brunoise, Marcona almonds; baked, rolled thin while hot.
- **Rack of Lamb** (400 g) — rosemary-wrapped bones, sous vide 62°C/144°F to core 57°C/135°F (~40 min), rested, seared fat-side down.

**Standout technique:** pressure-cooking crème fraîche with baking soda to force Maillard browning chemically; a savory nougatine (confectionery isomalt technique) built around garlic and almonds.

**Plating:** chops seared fat-side down, sliced between ribs; plated with caramelized crème fraîche, a tapenade quenelle, an oyster beignet, three pickled garlic cloves, warm garlic-rosemary jus, and a garlic nougatine shard; garnished with wild garlic blossoms.

#### 5. Blanquette de Veau
*Veal sweetbreads, constructed cream, kaolin clay, licorice*

Reinterprets the classically "white" braised-veal-and-cream dish by making it "whiter than white" (even the carrots are white): since butter yellows cream sauce, rendered veal marrow fat is instead **homogenized directly into white veal stock** to build a nondairy, kosher-compatible "constructed" veal cream whose fine fat droplets scatter light intensely. **Yield** 4 | **Equipment** sous vide, rotor-stator or ultrahigh-pressure homogenizer | **Time** 13 h.

- **Sous Vide Veal Sweetbreads** (500 g) — milk-soaked 3× over 12 h to purge, sous vide 67°C/153°F 1 h, glazed in a veal-stock/sherry/licorice-root/fennel-seed/star-anise reduction with **kaolin clay**.
- **Rice Crisp** (350 g) — sushi rice cooked to porridge, mixed with glutinous-rice-flour slurry, dehydrated 50°C/122°F 3 h, deep-fried 190°C/375°F.
- **Milk Foam** (500 g) — skim milk sous vide-infused 85°C/185°F 1 h with leek/mushroom/turnip/onion/garlic/thyme; stabilized with sucrose esters (Sucro) and Methocel F50, hand-blended into foam.
- **Glazed White Carrot** (300 g) — sous vide 85°C/185°F 45 min, reduced to a glaze with butter/fructose.
- **Sous Vide Turnip** (200 g) — sous vide 85°C/185°F 20 min with butter.
- **Constructed Veal Cream** (360 g) — tapioca starch, whey protein isolate, iota carrageenan, acetic acid, polysorbate 80, xanthan gum homogenized into cold white veal stock at 85°C/185°F, then rendered veal marrow fat drizzled in while homogenizing until fully emulsified.

**Standout technique:** a polysorbate-80/whey-protein/carrageenan emulsifier system homogenized with veal fat to build a nondairy "cream" sauce via fat-droplet light scattering; kaolin clay as an edible mineral flavoring agent.

**Plating:** sweetbreads basted in the clay-licorice glaze, sliced 2.5 cm thick; plated with milk foam, glazed carrots, turnips, topped with rice crisps; veal cream served alongside.

#### 6. Choucroute Royale
*Alder smoke, gin flavors, Munster, black trumpet mushroom*

Traces sauerkraut fermentation science (*Leuconostoc mesenteroides* colonizing salted, submerged cabbage, converting sugars to lactic acid before other bacteria finish the sour aroma). **Yield** 4 | **Equipment** pressure cooker, vacuum reduction equipment (sous vide/cold smoker optional) | **Time** 72 h (3 weeks if curing your own sauerkraut).

- **Juniper Brine** (600 g) — a gin-botanical brine: juniper, coriander, lemon/bitter-orange zest, angelica root, roasted malt, hops, cinnamon, ginger, allspice.
- **Pork Tenderloin with Juniper** (500 g) — brined 24 h, wrapped in a gelatin-infused leek sheet bonded with **Activa RM (transglutaminase)** to form an edible "skin," steamed, rested 12 h before sous vide.
- **Aromatic Alsatian Mustard** (optional, 245 g) — triple-blanched mustard seed (removes bitterness) + cider vinegar; keeps indefinitely.
- **Smoked Pork Cheek** (240 g) — **cold-smoked at 7°C/45°F, 50% RH (wet-bulb 3°C/37°F), 24 h** with alder/cherry wood, then sous vide 68°C/154°F 48 h.
- **Pork Trotter Beignet** (400 g) — trotter/snout **pressure-cooked at 1 bar/15 psi 2.5 h**, combined with black trumpet mushroom and ham, bound with Methocel K100M, breaded, deep-fried.
- **Braised Sauerkraut** (480 g) — cabbage + rutabaga sauerkraut braised with vegetable stock, dry Riesling, Muscat, and a bay/juniper/clove/coriander sachet.
- **Sweet Potato Fondant** (200 g) — satsuma-imo sous vide 95°C/203°F 45 min.
- **Alsatian Munster Sausage** (1.9 kg) — emulsion-style sausage (pork shoulder/fat ground cold, kept below 5–10°C to prevent overchopping), bound with Insta Cure No. 1, grated Munster, and quatre épices; sous vide to core 59°C/138°F.

**Standout technique:** Activa-bonded gelatin/leek "skin" around the tenderloin; wet-bulb-controlled cold-smoking of the cheek; a gin-botanical brine drawing an explicit parallel between juniper in gin and in pork brines.

**Plating:** sauerkraut spooned into bowls, dressed with sliced sausage, pork cheek, tenderloin, and sweet potato fondant; trotter beignets fried and served alongside with the Alsatian mustard.

**Chapter-wide hydrocolloids/additives:** bromelain, xanthan gum, guar gum, 160 Bloom gelatin, low-acyl gellan (Kelcogel F), sodium citrate, iota/kappa carrageenan, Ultra-Sperse A/3, sucrose esters (Sucro), Methocel F50/E4M/K100M, polysorbate 80, whey protein isolate, kaolin clay, Insta Cure No. 1, Activa RM, N-Zorbit M, konjac gum.

#### 2. Pot-au-Feu
*Beef shank rillette, pickled veal tongue, rare flatiron steak, crispy ravigote*

Traces pot-au-feu from its 1867 Larousse-canonized status as the French national dish through reinterpretations by Alain Chapel, Michel Guérard, and Eric Ripert. **Yield** 4 | **Equipment** sous vide, pressure cooker | **Time** 4 days.

- **Glazed Oxtail** (325 g) — sous vide **60°C/140°F for 100 h**; juices reduced to a glaze.
- **Sous Vide Veal Tongue** (250 g) — blanched, sous vide 70°C/158°F 24 h in a cider-vinegar/vegetable brine; liquid reduced to a syrup.
- **Rare Flatiron Steak** (500 g) — two pieces bonded with **Activa RM/GS**, rested 6 h, sous vide 55°C/131°F for 12 h (extendable to 24–48 h for more tenderness).
- **Beignet of Sauce Ravigote** (300 g, inspired by Wylie Dufresne) — a ravigote sauce solidified: milk fluid gel (gelatin + low-acyl gellan) blended with walnut oil, sous vide egg yolk, Dijon, vermouth vinegar, sea beans; cast, cubed, breaded, deep-fried.
- **Beef Shank Rillette** (365 g) — shank sous vide 85°C/185°F 5 h, shredded, folded with an agar/gelatin beef-stock fluid gel and warm rendered suet.
- **Pot-au-Feu Consommé** (200 g) — oxtail/shank roasted with aromatics, **pressure-cooked at 1 bar/15 psi for 2 h**, clarified, reduced, finished with sherry vinegar.
- **Clove Oil** (100 g) — grapeseed oil + 0.5% clove essential oil.
- **Sous Vide Root Vegetables** — baby leek, parisienne carrot/celery root/rutabaga/turnip, pearl onion, each individually sous vide 85°C/185°F 25–40 min.
- **Microwave-Fried Parsley and Carrot Tops** — brushed with oil, microwaved to crisp.

**Standout technique:** Activa-bonded double-thickness flatiron steak; a solid, fried "beignet" version of a classic liquid sauce.

**Plating:** tongue, oxtail, seared flatiron, and warm rillette arranged per plate with root vegetables and fried herb garnish, clove oil drizzle; consommé and beignets served tableside.

#### 3. Hungarian Beef Goulash
*Wagyu beef cheek, sour cream csipetke, compressed dill-pickled vegetables*

Opens with Károly Gundel's warning against foreign chefs "ruining" goulash and lays out gulyás orthodoxy (no flour, no wine, no rice) — which the authors admit to gently bending with black cumin and red wine vinegar. **Yield** 4 | **Equipment** sous vide (centrifuge optional) | **Time** 73 h.

- **Goulash Broth** (1 kg) — tomato confit, red pepper/onion, brown beef stock, toasted caraway/peppercorn, sweet+hot paprika, black cumin, sherry vinegar; sous vide 85°C/185°F 2.5 h, then **centrifuged at 27,500g for 1 h**.
- **American Wagyu Beef Cheek** (450 g) — sous vide **62°C/144°F for 72 h** in the broth.
- **Smoked Pepper Purée** (250 g) — broth + red pepper/onion/ham/smoked paprika sous vide 85°C/185°F 2 h, thickened with Ultra-Sperse 5.
- **Goulash Glaze** (75 g) — broth reduced 400 g → 75 g.
- **Compressed Dill-Pickled Vegetables** (300 g) — red pepper, cucumber, hon-shimeji, pearl onion, individually vacuum-brined.
- **Sour Cream Spaetzle (Csipetke)** — sautéed in clarified butter.
- **Rye Bread** (adapted from Horst Bandel, 1.25 kg) — multi-day sourdough: starter ripened 10–12 h at 21°C/70°F, rye berries sous vide 90°C/194°F 5 h, baked to core 99°C/210°F in a covered cast-iron mold.

**Standout technique:** 27,500g centrifuge clarification of the broth; 72-hour Wagyu cheek.

**Plating:** beef cheek sliced, brushed with glaze, plated over smoked pepper purée; garnished with pickled vegetables; csipetke and toasted rye bread on the side; broth poured tableside.

#### 5. American BBQ
Component-only extraction (title/headnote not recovered) — a full regional survey.

- **Rubs:** Memphis Rub, Kansas Rub.
- **Regional sauces:** Kansas City (ketchup-based), East Texas (ancho-chili/onion-butter), Memphis (tomato/cider vinegar/Worcestershire), North Carolina Eastern (vinegar-only, no tomato), South Carolina (Dijon-mustard/lager base), Kentucky (ketchup/molasses/pineapple/bourbon, for mutton), North Carolina Lexington-style (vinegar+ketchup dip), and a **Centrifuged BBQ Sauce** (tomato confit + beef jus, **centrifuged at 27,500g 1 h**, decanted clear). Sidebar: 0.2% xanthan gum improves sauce cling to meat.
- **Proteins**, all smoked then sous vide-finished: **Pulled Pork Shoulder** (smoked 65°C/149°F, 60% RH, 7 h; sous vide 65°C/150°F 72 h); **Pork Ribs** (same smoke; sous vide 60°C/140°F 48 h; torch-flashed); **Beef Short Ribs** (same smoke; sous vide 60°C/140°F 72 h); **Beef Brisket** (same smoke; sous vide 63°C/146°F 72 h — noted for its "highly developed smoke ring").

## 4. Cross-Volume Technique Index (Highlights)

A few techniques recur across the whole volume and mark it as a signature Modernist Cuisine collection:

- **Centrifugation (typically 27,500g)** for clarifying stocks, juices, and sauces — used in at least 9 dishes across Tender Cuts, Tough Cuts, Shellfish, Starches, and Fruits/Vegetables.
- **Activa (transglutaminase) bonding** — welding skin back onto meat (Crispy Hay-Smoked Chicken, Pigeon en Salmis), bonding two cuts into one uniform portion (Rare Flatiron Steak, Skate pavé, Paella rabbit loin, Tuna/escolar checkerboard), and firming low-gluten doughs (Russian Pelmeni).
- **Spherification** (standard and reverse) — Skate's caper "droplets," Shanghai Soup Dumplings' reverse-spherified soup spheres.
- **Liquid nitrogen** — cryosearing (Autumn Harvest Pork Roast, Crispy Hay-Smoked Chicken), cryopoaching a meringue (Salmon Rus), and flash-freezing produce for clean cellular separation (Thai Crab Miang's pomelo, Pulpo a la Gallega's chorizo).
- **Freeze-drying** — an entire dish built around it (Astronaut Ramen), plus garnishes throughout (onion powder, scallop powder, corn, strawberry powder).
- **Pressure cooking (commonly 1 bar/15 psi gauge)** for fast stock extraction and for forcing reactions (Maillard-browning crème fraîche in Rack of Lamb, per-grain texture control in Pigeon en Salmis).
- **Precision sous vide egg cookery** — 67°C/153°F yolk pasteurization (Breakfast Egg, Astronaut Ramen), 72°C/162°F scrambled-egg custard (Mushroom Omelet), 62°C/143°F duck-egg liquid center (Oeufs en Meurette).
- **Whipping-siphon aeration** used for both cold and warm foams (Suet Mousseline, Cauliflower Foam), tempura batters (Fish and Chips, Shellfish Omakase), and even fried "beignet" solids of what would classically be a sauce (Pot-au-Feu's ravigote beignet).

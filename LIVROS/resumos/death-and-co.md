# Death & Co

**Author:** David Kaplan, with Alex Day and Nick Fauchald; foreword by Toby Cecchini; photography by William Hereford
**Publisher:** Ten Speed Press, 2014
**Pages:** 573

## Structural Overview

The book documents the cocktail program of Death & Co, the influential East Village (NYC) speakeasy-style bar that opened in 2007 and became a driving force of the modern craft-cocktail revival. It is organized in five parts:

1. **Introduction** — the bar's history and philosophy (Dave Kaplan and Alex Day founding story, the role of head bartenders Phil Ward, Brian Miller, Thomas Waugh, Jillian Vose).
2. **Part One: Ingredients** — deep-dive chapters on spirits (gin, vodka, tequila/mezcal, whiskey, rum, brandy, aquavit), modifiers (vermouths, amari, liqueurs), bitters, sweeteners, juices, dairy/eggs, garnishes, and ice.
3. **Part Two: Tools** — bar equipment (shakers, strainers, jiggers, bar spoons, muddlers, mixing glasses, julep tins, swizzle sticks) and glassware.
4. **Part Three: Technique** — jiggering/measuring, shaking, stirring, straining, muddling, rimming, rinsing glasses, garnishing, building rounds, and ice science.
5. **Part Four: Creating New Cocktails** — the house creative methodology, including the "Mr. Potato Head" method, balance theory, and the four core strategies for building original drinks.
6. **Part Five: The Recipes** — roughly 500 pages of recipes, organized by base spirit and technique (e.g., "Gin, Shaken," "Gin, Stirred," "Rum, Shaken," "Rum, Stirred," "Agave, Shaken," "Agave, Stirred," "Whiskey," "Brandy," "Sparkling Cocktails," "Fortified Wine Cocktails," "Punch," "Juleps," "Flips and Fizzes," "Swizzles," "Multiple Choice," "Aquavit"), followed by thematic **Variation** chapters devoted to five classic templates: **Sazerac Variations, Negroni Variations, Daiquiri Variations, Manhattan Variations, Old-Fashioned Variations** (including a "Death & Co Family Daiquiri Album" of personal specs from alumni).
7. **Appendices** — Appendix One: Sweeteners, Infusions & Other Concoctions (syrups, infusions, batches, rims, miscellany); Appendix Two: The Death & Co Bookshelf (recommended reading); Appendix Three: Resources (suppliers/vendors).

Each recipe entry gives the drink's name, creator and year, sometimes a short anecdote, then an exact ingredient list (in ounces/teaspoons) followed by a one- to two-sentence method (shake/stir, glass, garnish).

## Philosophy & Technique Synthesis

- **Balance is the organizing principle.** A cocktail is treated as an equation of spirit, sweet, sour/acid, and dilution (via ice/water) that must reach equilibrium; bitters and modifiers fine-tune rather than dominate.
- **The "Mr. Potato Head" method:** build a reliable ratio/template (e.g., 2:¾:½ spirit:modifier:something-interesting, as in "Coin Toss") and swap the base spirit while keeping the supporting structure — proof that a good formula transcends any one spirit. The "Multiple Choice" chapter exists specifically to demonstrate this (one recipe printed with 4–6 alternate base spirits).
- **Four strategies for building new cocktails** (introduced in "Creating New Classics"): (1) riff on a classic (add/replace one ingredient in a known template — Manhattan, Old-Fashioned, Daiquiri, Negroni, Sazerac all get their own "Variations" chapters); (2) build from a flavor combination or inspiration (a food pairing, a place, a memory); (3) use a signature in-house ingredient (infusion, syrup, or "batch") as the anchor; (4) reverse-engineer from name/concept.
- **Stirred vs. shaken** is a deliberate technical choice: stirred drinks (all-spirit/fortified-wine combinations) are chilled and diluted gently to preserve texture and clarity; shaken drinks (containing citrus, dairy, egg, or puree) need the aeration and rapid dilution of hard shaking, and eggs/dairy get a "dry shake" (no ice) first to build foam before shaking with ice.
- **Punch requires five elements** by house rule: spirit, sugar, citrus, water, and spice — spice (via tea infusions, bitters, or fresh spice) is what technically makes it a "punch" rather than a large cocktail; the classic method is muddling sugar cubes with citrus and club soda, then stirring in the rest over ice in a pitcher before straining over a large block in a punch bowl.
- **Juleps and swizzles** get their own controlled builds: julep tins are packed with crushed ice in stages (half full → stir/churn 10 sec → two-thirds → stir again → cone above rim) until the tin frosts, with mint reserved as a bouquet garnish rather than muddled in; swizzles are built the same way in a pilsner glass, ideally swizzled with a bois lélé stick, with bitters often "swizzled on top" as a final aromatic layer rather than mixed in.
- **In-house infrastructure**: extensive use of infused spirits/vermouths (tea, chile, fruit, herb infusions), proprietary syrups (Cinnamon Bark, Demerara, Ginger, Acacia Honey, Vanilla, Cane Sugar), house bitters blends (House Orange Bitters, House Peychaud's Bitters, House Sweet Vermouth), and "batches" (premixed multi-ingredient bottles like Donn's Mix #1, Bergerac Mix, Pendennis Mix, Zombie Mix) kept behind the bar in "cheater bottles" for speed and precision.
- **Orgeat, horchata, and other house dairy/nut bases** are made from scratch (toasted almond milk-based orgeat with cognac, amaretto, and rose water).
- The daiquiri is treated as the bar's "secret handshake" — the truest test of a bartender's skill (balance of sweet/sour/booze, properly cold, not overdiluted); this belief motivates the extensive "Daiquiri Variations" chapter and the personal-spec "Family Album."

## Sample Recipes (organized by category)

### Classic-template variations

**Naked and Famous** (Joaquín Simó, 2011) — a mezcal Last Word/Paper Plane hybrid
- ¾ oz Del Maguey Chichicapa mezcal
- ¾ oz yellow Chartreuse
- ¾ oz Aperol
- ¾ oz lime juice
- Shake with ice, strain into a coupe. No garnish.

**White Negroni** (Alex Day, 2009)
- 1½ oz Ford's gin
- ¾ oz Dolin blanc vermouth
- ¾ oz Suze Saveur d'Autrefois liqueur
- Stir over ice, strain into a double rocks glass over 1 large ice cube. Garnish with lemon twist.

**Oaxaca Old-Fashioned** (Phil Ward, 2008) — one of the most influential modern classics
- 1½ oz El Tesoro reposado tequila
- ½ oz Del Maguey San Luis del Rio mezcal
- 1 teaspoon agave nectar
- 2 dashes Angostura bitters
- Stir over ice, strain into a coupe. Flame an orange twist over the drink, then drop it in.

**Angus Story Daiquiri** (Phil Ward, 2012) — three-rum daiquiri
- ¾ oz Appleton Estate V/X rum
- ¾ oz Ron del Barrilito 3-star rum
- ½ oz La Favorite rhum agricole blanc
- ¾ oz lime juice
- ½ oz Cane Sugar Syrup
- Shake with ice, strain into a coupe. Garnish with lime wedge.

**Mi Amaro** (Joaquín Simó, 2009) — Manhattan-template tequila drink
- 2 oz El Tesoro reposado tequila
- ½ oz Amaro CioCiaro
- ½ oz Carpano Antica Formula vermouth
- ¼ oz St-Germain
- Stir over ice, strain into a coupe. Garnish with grapefruit twist.

### Gin / Agave / Rum / Brandy / Whiskey — shaken and stirred

**Naked and Famous, Fresa Brava** (Phil Ward, 2009) — muddled tequila sour
- 1 strawberry
- 2 oz jalapeño-infused Siembra Azul blanco tequila
- ¾ oz yellow Chartreuse
- ¾ oz lemon juice
- ½ oz Simple Syrup
- Muddle strawberry, add rest, shake with ice, double strain into coupe. No garnish.

**Green and Red** (Thomas Waugh, 2012) — stirred cucumber cocktail
- 3 cucumber wheels (muddled)
- 1 oz Siete Leguas blanco tequila
- 1 oz jalapeño-infused Siembra Azul blanco tequila
- ¾ oz Cocchi Americano
- ¼ oz Dolin dry vermouth
- ¼ oz Alvear Festival pale cream sherry
- Muddle cucumber in mixing glass, add rest, stir over ice, strain into coupe. Garnish with cucumber ribbon.

**Widow's Laurel** (Joaquín Simó, 2009) — riff on the Widow's Kiss
- 2 oz Busnel VSOP Calvados
- ½ oz Drambuie
- ½ oz Carpano Antica Formula vermouth
- 1 teaspoon St. Elizabeth Allspice Dram
- 1 dash Angostura bitters
- Stir over ice, strain into coupe. Garnish with 3 brandied cherries.

**Jekyll and Hyde** (Thomas Waugh, 2009) — Old-Fashioned variation
- 1½ oz Eagle Rare 10-year bourbon
- ½ oz Laird's Bonded apple brandy
- ½ teaspoon Cinnamon Bark Syrup
- 1 teaspoon Demerara Syrup
- 2 dashes Bitter Truth Aromatic bitters + 2 dashes Angostura bitters
- Stir over ice, strain into double rocks glass over 1 large ice cube. Garnish with lemon and orange twists.

### Sparkling / Fortified Wine

**Bitter French** (Phil Ward, 2008) — French 75 riff
- 1 oz Plymouth gin
- ¼ oz Campari
- ½ oz lemon juice
- ½ oz Simple Syrup
- Dry champagne to top
- Shake all but champagne with ice, strain into flute, top with champagne. Squeeze grapefruit twist over and discard.

**Café Sandinista** (Joaquín Simó, 2012) — fortified-wine cocktail
- 1 oz Lustau East India Solera sherry
- ¼ oz coffee- and chile-infused Campari
- ½ oz orange juice
- ½ oz lime juice
- ½ oz Demerara Syrup
- Small pinch kosher salt
- Short shake with 3 ice cubes, strain into double rocks glass over crushed ice. Garnish with grated coffee bean.

### Punch

**Mother's Ruin Punch** (Phil Ward, 2008) — the bar's original punch, serves 4-6
- 8 white sugar cubes
- 2 oz club soda (for muddling)
- 4 oz Plymouth gin
- 2 oz Cinnamon Orange Tea–Infused Sweet Vermouth
- 4 oz grapefruit juice
- 2 oz lemon juice
- 3 oz dry champagne
- Garnish: 6 grapefruit wheels
- Muddle sugar cubes with club soda in pitcher until dissolved; add remaining ingredients (except champagne), fill three-quarters with ice, stir until cold, strain into punch bowl over a large ice block, top with champagne.

### Julep / Swizzle

**Maple Julep** (Phil Ward, 2008)
- 2 oz Old Overholt rye
- ¼ oz maple syrup
- Garnish: mint bouquet
- Build in julep tin, pack with crushed ice in stages, stirring until frosted; garnish with mint bouquet in center of ice cone.

**6th Street Swizzle** (Phil Ward, 2008)
- 1½ oz La Favorite rhum agricole blanc
- 1 oz lime juice
- ¾ oz Cane Sugar Syrup
- 2 dashes Angostura bitters
- Garnish: mint sprig, lime wheel
- Whip ingredients with crushed ice pellets, pour into pilsner glass filled with crushed ice, swizzle until cold.

### Flips / Fizzes

**Sleepy Hollow Fizz** (Brian Miller, 2008)
- 1½ oz Flor de Caña 7-year rum
- ½ oz El Dorado high-strength 151 rum
- ½ oz lemon juice
- ½ oz maple syrup
- 1 egg yolk
- 2 teaspoons pumpkin puree
- Club soda to top
- Dry shake, then shake again with ice; strain into fizz glass, top with club soda.

### Appendix staples (house syrups & infusions)

- **Cane Sugar Syrup:** 2 cups organic cane sugar to 1 cup water, cooked without boiling until dissolved.
- **Cinnamon Bark Syrup:** muddle 1 oz cassia cinnamon sticks, add 2 cups water + 2 cups superfine sugar, boil, simmer 4 min, steep overnight, strain.
- **Ginger Syrup:** ½ cup fresh ginger juice blended with 1 cup superfine sugar.
- **Grenadine (house):** 4 cups unfiltered pomegranate juice + 3 cups cane sugar, cooked, then stirred with 6 oz pomegranate molasses and orange-twist oils.
- **Orgeat:** toasted almond milk + sugar cooked together, finished with cognac, amaretto, and rose water.
- **House Peychaud's Bitters (batch):** 2 parts Peychaud's Bitters to 1 part Bitter Truth Creole Bitters.
- **House Orange Bitters (batch):** equal parts Fee Brothers West Indian orange bitters, Regans' orange bitters, and Angostura orange bitters.

This gives a representative cross-section: classic-template variations, all major shaken/stirred spirit chapters, punch, julep, swizzle, flip/fizz formats, and the house infrastructure of syrups/bitters/infusions that underlies nearly every recipe in the book.

## Structural Overview (identical to the English edition)

1. **Introdução (pp. 7–36)**
   - *A história do Foodpairing* (p. 8) — Bernard Lahousse's first-person origin story: 2005 collaboration with Michelin chefs Sang-Hoon Degeimbre (Air du Temps) and Kobe Desramaults (In de Wulf); the discovery, via GC-MS run with engineer Jeroen Lammertyn, that kiwi and oysters share green/marine aldehydes — the "kiwitre" dish. Parallel discovery credited to François Benzi (Firmenich, 1992, jasmine/liver via indole) and Heston Blumenthal's caviar + white chocolate pairing (The Fat Duck) — both reproduced as sidebar essays in the authors' own words. Company founded 2009 with chef Peter Coucquyt and Kasteel Withof; by publication had 200,000+ members in 140+ countries and had analyzed 3,000+ ingredients.
   - *Aromas Chave* ("Key Aromas") — VOCs, GC-MS mechanics, odour-detection thresholds, key odorants (coffee's ~1,000 VOCs vs. ~30–40 perceptible key odorants), the 2005 New York maple-syrup-smell mystery (solved 2009, traced to a New Jersey fragrance plant's sotolon emissions) as a case study in odour thresholds, synthetic/gestalt aroma perception, and the four non-linear interaction types (synergy, inhibition, suppression, "ofuscamento"/overshadowing).
   - *Olfação versus Gustação* — orthonasal vs. retronasal smell, five basic tastes, trigeminal sensation (capsaicin, menthol, carbonation), supertasters (~25% of population, correlated with fungiform papillae density), Charles Zuker's Columbia University research on taste perception occurring in the brain, not the tongue.
   - *A Importância do Aroma* — evolutionary role of smell (rotten food detection, pregnancy-linked heightened smell), flavour-flavour learning via Pavlovian/evaluative conditioning, and a "sabor-sabor" (flavour-flavour) diagram showing how pairing a disliked ingredient with a liked one can build acceptance over time.
   - *Moléculas de aroma* — precursors (carbohydrates, amino acids, fatty acids), Maillard reaction (~140°C) and caramelization (~160°C), fermentation; the 10-category aroma "building blocks" taxonomy: Aldeídos, Álcoois, Cetonas, Ésteres, Lactonas, Ácidos, Terpenos, Furanos/furanoides, Furanonas, Fenóis — each illustrated with named example molecules (hexanal, geosmina, beta-damascenona, etil butanoato, whisky lactone, ácido acético, limoneno/mentol/linalol, sotolon, furaneol, eugenol).
   - *Construindo sua biblioteca de aromas* — palate/nose training methodology, sommelier reference-library analogy, aroma-and-memory (amygdala/hippocampus pathway), a home "smell-recognition exercise."
   - *Como funciona o Foodpairing* — the proprietary **14 tipos de aroma / 70 descritores** taxonomy (Frutado, Cítricos, Floral, Verde, Ervas, Vegetal, Caramelo, Assado, Maluco [= "Nutty," mistranslated literally as "Maluco"/crazy rather than "Amendoado"/nutty — another machine-translation slip], Woody, Apimentado, Brega [= "Cheesy," bizarrely rendered as "Brega," Portuguese for "tacky/cheesy-in-the-kitsch-sense," a homonym error confusing culinary "cheesy" with slang "cheesy"], Animal, Químico); how to read an **Aroma Wheel** (roda de aromas: inner ring = 14 types present/absent, outer ring = descriptor concentration bands) and a **Pairing Grid** (grade de harmonização: bold main ingredient + up to 10 paired ingredients, each row of 14 coloured dots sized by shared-molecule strength); worked example using cooked quinoa.
   - *Da combinação de ingredientes a pratos deliciosos* — texture contrast (60 identified texture types, reduced to "macia" vs. "crocante" as the two master categories), taste contrast (5 basic tastes plus a diagram of which tastes reinforce/reduce/balance each other, e.g. salt reduces bitterness, fat reduces alcohol burn), and "complexidade percebida" (perceived complexity) — Daniel Berlyne's "unidade na variedade" (unity-in-variety) aesthetic principle, worked through five example ingredient-groups (A–E) culminating in Mexican mole de pollo as the paradigm of well-managed complexity.
2. **Ingredientes e combinações (pp. 39–371)** — the same ~90 ingredient chapters as the English edition, in identical order and pagination (see full list below), each with an aroma wheel, an ingredient essay, at least one named chef/bartender pairing sidebar, and multiple pairing grids.
3. **Índice de ingredientes (p. 372)**, Glossário de termos do Reino Unido e dos EUA, Notas de rodapé (endnotes), Agradecimentos (image/editor/author acknowledgements), Sobre Foodpairing / Sobre os autores (p. 387–388).

## Sample of pairing content read directly (Tequila chapter, pp. 80–83)

- **LaMiel** cocktail (Tony Conigliaro, Drink Factory, London) — tequila + buckwheat honey ("mel de trigo sarraceno") + black tea + jasmine flower/tea, chosen because the tea's tannic structure gives the drink body and the buckwheat honey's earthy sweetness echoes the tequila's own earthy/smoky agave notes, balanced by jasmine's floral lift.
- Aroma-wheel breakdown of blanco/silver tequila: dominant Floral (rose is the single largest wedge), Fruity (tropical, pineapple, banana, apple, lemon), Earthy/Woody notes from agave cooking, plus Vegetable (bell pepper, mushroom, potato/onion) and Spicy (clove) — the rose/floral dominance is explicitly attributed to shared aroma molecules with the agave plant's cooking/roasting process.
- Pairing grids given for buckwheat honey, pulque (traditional low-ABV fermented agave sap drink, called *metoctli* in Nahuatl — "metl" = agave, "octli" = wine), jasmine tea, Gruyère, dates ("data"), hibiscus flower, horseradish purée, and grapefruit juice, plus a Don Julio Blanco-specific grid (dates, hibiscus flower, coriander leaves, dry-cured ham, pancake, Gruyère, boiled beetroot, yuzu, Taiwanese fish sauce, pan-fried wild duck).
- Two "classic pairing" callouts: **tequila + horseradish** (the logic behind the Bloody Maria, the tequila variant of the Bloody Mary — tomato juice, lime, Worcestershire, Tabasco, horseradish, celery, pepper) and **tequila + citrus fruit** (explained via shared linalool concentrations between lime and tequila, underlying the Margarita — tequila, triple sec, lime juice — and the Paloma — tequila, lime juice, grapefruit soda).
